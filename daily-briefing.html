🇺🇸 United States — a landmark power ruling, a Fed reprieve, a term's final day

Supreme CourtHumphrey's Executor falls: 6–3 ruling expands the president's firing power

In one of the most consequential separation-of-powers rulings in decades, the Court struck down the 91-year-old Humphrey's Executor precedent (6–3), which since 1935 had shielded members of independent agencies (FTC, FCC, NLRB and the like) from at-will removal. Presidents may now fire those officials without cause, sharply concentrating control over the federal bureaucracy in the White House. (Confirmed; NPR, Washington Post, CNN.)

Federal ReserveBut the Fed is spared: Lisa Cook stays, 5–4

In a closely watched carve-out, the Court declined (5–4) to let Trump immediately remove Fed Governor Lisa Cook, ruling she can remain in her seat until the underlying litigation is resolved in the lower courts — preserving, at least for now, the perception of central-bank independence even as the broader doctrine shifts. (Confirmed; CNN, NPR.)

electionsCourt upholds Mississippi's mail-ballot grace period; Trump pushes the "SAVE America Act"

The justices upheld a Mississippi law letting officials count mail ballots postmarked by Election Day but received up to five days later. Trump responded by urging Congress to pass the "SAVE America Act" — strict voter-ID and proof-of-citizenship requirements plus tighter limits on mail voting. (Confirmed; CNN.)

todayThe term's final day: birthright citizenship and three more cases

The Court holds the last day of its term today (Tuesday, June 30), with roughly four decisions outstanding — most prominently the administration's challenge to birthright citizenship. Watch for opinions to land through the morning. (Confirmed/pending; NPR, SCOTUSblog.)
