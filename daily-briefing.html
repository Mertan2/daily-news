🇺🇸 United States

Two US soldiers killed in Iranian barrage on Jordan; CENTCOM runs an eighth straight night of strikes

Two US service members died and a third is missing after Iranian ballistic missiles and drones struck as partner forces defended positions in Jordan — the first US military deaths since this phase of the war reopened on June 27. CENTCOM said it would withhold the fallen soldiers' identities until 24 hours after next-of-kin notification, and reported completing its eighth consecutive night of strikes against dozens of Iranian military targets. By independent tallies, roughly 50 people have been killed in Iran and more than 500 injured since June 27; overnight strikes were said to have destroyed a maritime control tower at the port of Chabahar in the Gulf of Oman.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it targeted US bases in Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman; Kuwait reported an Iranian strike on a power and desalination plant. President Trump has reportedly been presented options to widen the operation, whose stated objective — loosening Iran's grip on the Strait of Hormuz — remains open-ended. Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei called Trump's signature on last month's framework "worthless and invalid," accusing Washington of "repeated breaches."

🇪🇺 Europe

France and Germany open nuclear cooperation as Europe hedges against Washington

Chancellor Friedrich Merz and President Emmanuel Macron announced that German conventional forces will take part in a French-led nuclear exercise before the end of 2026 — with French Rafales (nuclear-capable) and German Eurofighters already flying joint refuelling drills. The move, unveiled around the annual Franco-German summit in Brühl, is the most concrete step yet toward European nuclear self-reliance amid doubts over the future of the US security guarantee. The UK, Poland, the Netherlands, Belgium, Greece, Sweden, Denmark and Norway have all signalled interest in the French initiative.

Why the timing: both capitals are racing to lock in cooperation before Macron's term ends, uncertain whether his successor will share the same commitment to deeper European integration.

Burnham becomes UK prime minister Monday — and scraps the digital-ID scheme first

Confirmed unopposed as Labour leader on July 17, former Manchester mayor Andy Burnham is set to be appointed prime minister on Monday, July 20 — Britain's seventh in a decade, and installed without a general election. Watch: his first cabinet, whether he inherits Starmer's fiscal rules, and how a left-populist pitch survives contact with a bond market that has already forced Britain into uncomfortable interventions. UK June CPI is due July 22.

Commission proposes an ETS overhaul — framed as competitiveness, read by critics as bending the Green Deal

On July 17 the European Commission proposed a targeted revision of the Emissions Trading System, pitched as aligning the carbon market with the EU's 90%-by-2040 goal: a new Industrial Decarbonisation Bank, an "Investment Booster," ring-fencing of at least half of future ETS revenue for industrial decarbonisation, updated free-allocation and Carbon Border Adjustment (CBAM) rules, and a gradual extension to municipal waste incineration.

Two readings of the same text: Brussels presents this as strengthening the ETS as an investment engine. Industry-friendly member states and green campaigners read the accompanying flexibilities — softer cap trajectory and extended free allowances flagged in recent weeks — as a real-world loosening of the Green Deal's core instrument. Both framings are live; the fight moves to Parliament and Council.

🇨🇳 China

Into an end-July Politburo, Beijing weighs easing language over a big-bang stimulus

After Q2 GDP grew 4.3% — missing the ~4.5% consensus and Beijing's own target for the first time since Covid, with H1 fixed-asset investment down 5.7% and property investment down ~18% — attention turns to an end-July Politburo meeting. Analysts expect stronger easing rhetoric and a push for local governments to accelerate projects and "moderately frontload" Q4 debt quotas into Q3, rather than a large new package: roughly RMB 6.8 trillion of this year's approved bond issuance remained unused at end-June. 🇯🇵 Japan

A bond market disciplines a spending government: 10-year JGB near a 30-year high, yen past ¥162

The benchmark 10-year JGB yield hit 2.901% on July 9 — its highest since 1996 — and trades around 2.78%, up more than 70 basis points this year, as markets react to BOJ normalisation and to PM Sanae Takaichi's fiscal turn. After her cabinet's draft "Honebuto" guidelines effectively abandoned consolidation targets, yields spiked to a 30-year high and the yen slid past ¥162 per dollar. The government's roadmap outlines more than ¥370 trillion in public-private investment through FY2040 across 17 strategic sectors.

The pressure point: a former policymaker warns the BOJ could face political pressure to ramp up bond-buying if the 10-year breaks the 3% threshold — the level at which Takaichi's spending program and the central bank's independence collide most directly.
