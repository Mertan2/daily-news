Neurotech · Milestone

Paradromics performs first in-human BCI implant at Michigan

At the University of Michigan today, Paradromics completed the first successful human implantation of the Connexus BCI — a dime-sized 420-micro-electrode device placed in the temporal lobe of a consenting epilepsy patient already undergoing neurosurgery. Dr. Oren Sagher performed the surgery; Dr. Matthew Willsey led the research placement. Implant + removal time: ~20 minutes. The device recorded neural activity at single-neuron resolution. This formally puts Paradromics into clinical-stage operations and turns the previously one-sided Neuralink race into a two-horse contest at the bandwidth frontier; Connexus reports >200 bps decoding (≈20× Neuralink's initial demo). Neuralink itself disclosed in April that 45 participants are now enrolled across three continents, with 85% of spinal-injury participants hitting pick-and-place tasks within 150% of non-injured baselines. (Michigan Medicine · Paradromics · Nature · TechFundingNews)
