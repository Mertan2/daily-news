LeadIran, Hormuz, and the ammunition ledger

Munitions"Virtually all" of the long-range stockpile is gone

Reuters reported on 4 August that the US Army has used up most of its Army Tactical Missile System and Precision Strike Missile inventory across five months of the Iran campaign, with two sources saying virtually all had been expended. A fourth source said Central Command is drawing down stocks pre-positioned in other theatres to reload — so the global reserve is shrinking, not merely the regional one.

CNN reported the same day that the military has burned through ~80% of its THAAD interceptor inventory relative to pre-war levels, and roughly half of its Patriot interceptors. Even at quadrupled production, meaningful restocking is projected to run into 2027 or beyond. Commanders have been rationing interceptors and selectively defending against Iranian strikes — a decision reporting links directly to the President's repeated pauses on further mass attacks.

Trump responded Thursday by insisting the US has "vast quantities of ammunition" and that defence contractors are "establishing the largest number of factories and production facilities in US history." The White House separately denied a report concerning the President and Defense Secretary Hegseth: "What was written… never happened at all."

EuropeKyiv, Ceuta, and a continent on fire

Ukraine24 ballistic missiles, zero interceptions, 17 dead

Overnight into Wednesday, Russia fired 24 ballistic missiles, four cruise missiles and 115 drones at Kyiv and the surrounding region. Seventeen people were killed and 44 wounded. Ukraine intercepted no ballistic missiles at all. Targets included civilian enterprise storage sites, a railway station, a brewery, building-materials warehouses and civilian logistics facilities.

The Patriot is the only system in Ukraine's inventory capable of engaging ballistic munitions, and the global interceptor shortage — explicitly worsened by the Iran war — has hollowed out the stock available to Kyiv, alongside the wind-down of US aid. Trump told Zelensky at the July NATO summit that Washington would license domestic Ukrainian production; days later he told the Financial Times he was "not sure" the licence would come. Read this item and the munitions item above as a single story.

Separately on Sunday 3 August, drone debris struck a beach at Gelendzhik in Russia, killing seven people including three children.

MigrationCeuta: the ministers met, and the Parliament followed

JapanHiroshima at 81, and a fiscal-monetary collision

TodayThe 81st Peace Memorial Ceremony

At 8:15 this morning the peace bell rang and Hiroshima fell silent. Representatives of 121 countries and regions attended, with about 50,000 people in the Peace Memorial Park. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi and Mayor Kazumi Matsui spoke. The names of 4,393 victims whose deaths were confirmed over the past year were added to the cenotaph registry, bringing the total to 353,639.

Matsui's address went further than ceremonial: "Downplaying the inhumanity of nuclear weapons and accepting the use of force to pursue one's own prosperity risk cycles of violent retaliation that could ultimately result in another Hiroshima or Nagasaki." He called on political leaders to abandon nuclear deterrence as a realistic security doctrine — delivered in a year when a nuclear-threshold state is at war and the survivor population is thinning fast. Nagasaki marks its anniversary Sunday.

Science & HealthAn Ebola record, a lunar crater

OutbreakThe DRC's largest Ebola epidemic on record

As of 4 August (data through 3 August), the DRC has reported 3,874 confirmed cases and 1,751 deaths, with 717 patients hospitalised in isolation. Ituri accounts for 3,375 cases and 1,417 deaths across 28 of 36 health zones; North Kivu 426 cases and 290 deaths across 12 of 34. One recent reporting increment alone added 72 cases and 44 deaths.

This is now the largest Ebola outbreak ever recorded in the DRC and the second largest anywhere. Four weeks ago the count was 3,605 cases and 1,587 deaths; a month before that, 1,460 and 452. The species is Bundibugyo, against which the licensed vaccines and monoclonals — built for Zaire — are not certified. Remdesivir and MBP134 trials began 12 July; Oxford has Phase 1 clearance. Case fatality remains near 45%.

SpaceA Falcon 9 upper stage hit the Moon — and the plume was spectroscopically read

At 06:35 UTC on 5 August, a spent ~4-tonne Falcon 9 upper stage struck the lunar surface near Einstein Crater at about 2.43 km/s (~8,700 km/h), throwing regolith kilometres above the site. The impact was unplanned: the stage was left over from the 15 January 2025 launch that carried Firefly's Blue Ghost 1 and ispace's Resilience lander.

The scientifically interesting part is the observation. ESO's Very Large Telescope caught the impact and resolved sodium and lithium emission lines in the plume, persisting five to ten minutes — a rare directly-observed, roughly-known-energy impact experiment on the Moon, and a free calibration point for models of lunar exosphere generation.

Sources: CNN · EarthSky · Wikipedia · Project Pluto
