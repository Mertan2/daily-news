🇺🇸 United States

Iran war grinds into an eleventh night; Hegseth asks for $88bn as Senate forces another war-powers vote

The US carried out what CENTCOM described as an eleventh consecutive night of strikes on Iranian military facilities by July 22, after the June ceasefire — conditioned on reopening the Strait of Hormuz — collapsed. Trump warned Tehran would "pay" for recent American deaths; mediators Pakistan and Qatar have floated a 10-day ceasefire, but Washington has downplayed accepting it, with Rubio insisting any deal guarantee freedom of navigation through Hormuz. Secretary of War Pete Hegseth testified Tuesday that the war has cost about $38bn so far and pressed Congress for a nearly $88bn supplemental.

Two US service members were killed and one reported missing after an Iranian attack on a base in Jordan around July 17–18; roughly 100 personnel have suffered mostly minor injuries since early July. On Wednesday, Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer said Democrats will force another war-powers resolution "to end this unauthorized war"; a prior version cleared the Senate 50–48 in late June with four Republicans joining, but Congress has repeatedly failed to bind the administration.

Oil spikes toward $95 on Hormuz and Red Sea attacks

Brent crude surged roughly 3–4% to about $94–95 a barrel on July 22 — its highest since early June — driven by the eleventh night of US strikes and Houthi claims of striking two Saudi tankers in the Red Sea. 🇪🇺 Europe

Burnham opens with a cost-of-living package: zero VAT on electricity, a £2 bus cap, Digital ID scrapped

In office since July 20, UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham made cost of living his first move, telling his cabinet he leads a "cost-of-living government." VAT on domestic electricity bills will be cut from 5% to 0% from October 1 — an estimated £850m in 2026-27, funded by cancelling Starmer's Digital ID programme — alongside a £2 single bus-fare cap across England from January 2027, reversing Starmer's rise to £3. His cabinet took shape with John Healey as Chancellor, Ed Miliband as Foreign Secretary, and Yvette Cooper moving to Health; Burnham promised "the biggest changes in the last 40 years" and more power devolved from Westminster.

Context: Burnham is Britain's seventh prime minister in a decade, installed without a general election — so the early, tangible giveaways double as a bid for legitimacy the ballot box hasn't provided.

🇹🇷 Türkiye

TCMB holds at 37% — and with no August meeting, the decision stands until September 10

The Monetary Policy Committee kept the one-week repo rate at 37% on July 23 — its fourth consecutive hold — with the overnight lending rate at 40% and overnight borrowing at 35.5%. All 16 economists in the AA Finans and Bloomberg HT surveys had unanimously forecast a hold. Crucially, there is no committee meeting in August, so this decision governs markets through a two-month gap until September 10 (the remaining 2026 dates are Sept 10, Oct 22 and Dec 10). The statement said the tight stance will continue "until price stability is achieved," citing slowing activity and weak domestic demand, and flagged that July's monthly inflation may temporarily rise.
