2 Neurological / neuromorphic computers — BCI now a two-horse race

Paradromics joined Neuralink in humans this week

On May 14, Paradromics completed its first in-human implant of the Connexus BCI at the University of Michigan. The procedure: a dime-sized 421-microwire-electrode array placed in the temporal lobe of an epilepsy patient who was already in scheduled neurosurgery. Total implant + remove: ~20 minutes. Surgeon: Dr. Oren Sagher. Research lead: Dr. Matthew Willsey.

  • The Connexus device records at single-neuron resolution.
  • Published sheep-trial data show ~200 bps data throughput — roughly 25× Neuralink's claimed 8-bps cursor-control record.
  • Mechanism: an EpiPen-like inserter, vs. Neuralink's robotic insertion.
  • The FDA has already approved Paradromics for a long-term speech-restoration trial — two volunteers with neurological speech loss implanted earlier this year.

Neuralink's response

  • April disclosure: 45 enrolled patients across three continents.
  • ~85% of spinal-injury participants achieving pick-and-place at ≥150% of non-injured baselines.
  • Musk: high-volume production this year; next-gen surgical robot can reach virtually any brain region.

The neuromorphic side — chips that compute like brains

  • Cambridge hafnium-oxide chip (May 13): integrated synaptic devices that combine memory + processing in one place; ~70% reduction in AI energy compared with conventional GPUs running equivalent workloads.
  • University of Missouri team has a parallel push around brain-inspired hardware to address AI energy growth (projected to double by decade's end).
  • Academic context: the Biological Interface System to Cortex (BISC) — a single CMOS silicon chip thinned to 50 µm carrying tens of thousands of electrodes, 1,000+ recording channels and 16,000+ stimulation channels — is the third platform now circulating in the literature.
"Neuromorphic computing combines memory and processing in one place, similar to how the brain works. This method could cut energy use by as much as 70% while also allowing systems to learn and adapt more naturally."
What it means: Two things are happening at once. On the wet side, BCIs go from one player to two within ~30 days; Paradromics' 25× bps advantage is the real story, but the real test is the speech-restoration trial. On the silicon side, neuromorphic chips finally have a credible energy story (Cambridge's 70% cut) at a moment when AI data-center energy is projected to double by 2030. The intersection — brain-like silicon that can host real-time BCI decoding at the edge — is no longer a 5-year story.

3 Quantum computing — W-state, 50-qubit sim, exotic matter, Japan stack

The four breakthroughs of the past 10 days

  • May 11 — Jülich Supercomputing Centre + NVIDIA completed the first full simulation of a 50-qubit universal quantum computer using JUPITER, Europe's new exascale supercomputer. Smashes the previous 48-qubit record. Doubles the practical ceiling for classical verification of NISQ-era quantum claims.
  • May 12 — ETH Zürich: neutral-atom qubits made more stable under operation than ever before — error rates the lowest reported in the literature for atom-based platforms.
  • May 13 — Kyoto University + Hiroshima University: built a tabletop photonic circuit that performs one-shot identification of 3-photon W states by exploiting cyclic shift symmetry through a photonic quantum-Fourier-transform circuit. Generalises to any number of photons. Removes a long-standing bottleneck for scalable quantum networks.
  • May 15 — Fujitsu × Institute of Science Tokyo: joint quantum + HPC research cluster launched — full-stack qubit design and control plus talent pipeline. Crystallises Japan's national-champion play.

Error-correction context — "exotic matter" (May 4)

The May 4 result showed that "driving" materials with timed magnetic shifts can produce exotic quantum states that are far more stable and resistant to errors. If this scales, it's a direct attack on the central problem of quantum computing — qubit decoherence — and complements the Kyoto W-state work which is about readout at scale.

The standing benchmarks

  • Q-CTRL × IBM: 3,000× speedup on 120-qubit Fermi-Hubbard materials simulation with runtime error suppression — still the standing "useful quantum advantage" benchmark in materials science.
  • Google Willow: 13,000× over classical via Quantum Echoes (in Nature).
  • IBM Cleveland Clinic / RIKEN: 12,635-atom protein simulated via quantum-centric supercomputing.
  • Harvard team this month: "advancing faster than expected."

The Time piece this week

Time published an April-bridge piece quoting researchers warning the public "is not prepared" for the pace of AI-accelerated quantum advance: AI tools are speeding up algorithm design and error-correction code generation, compounding the platform-side progress. The flag: post-quantum cryptography migration timelines may be too slow.

What it means: Pre-2026, the bottlenecks were qubit stability, readout, and scaling. The past 10 days delivered concrete progress on all three (ETH on stability, Kyoto on readout, Jülich on simulation/verification). With Fujitsu × Sci-Tokyo crystallising Japan's stack, the geography is now genuinely three-cornered (US-led IBM/Google/Q-CTRL; Europe-led ETH/Jülich; Japan-led Fujitsu/RIKEN). Watch the "useful advantage" benchmark — that's the line that matters.

  • 3 deaths (2 lab-confirmed Andes hantavirus, 1 still suspected).

  • Two additional cases confirmed since the May 8 update — one from France, one from Spain.
  • No US cases. 18 American repatriated passengers monitored at the National Quarantine Unit at the University of Nebraska Medical Center.

  • 5 California residents under contact-monitoring.
  • No new clusters anywhere. No community spread.

Why Andes is special

Andes hantavirus is the only hantavirus species known to spread human-to-human — typically via close, sustained contact between people, possibly airborne in close-contact indoor settings. Case-fatality rate ~40%. Incubation period up to two months. Endemic in South America (mainly Argentina and Chile).

What happens next

  • The MV Hondius docks in the Netherlands May 17–18 — final passenger health screen at port.
  • The 42-day Andes surveillance window for repatriated passengers stays open into mid-June.
  • CDC HAN00528 Level 3 advisory holds.
  • Watch for: late-onset secondary cases among repatriated passenger contacts; any case at all outside the cruise-passenger circle (would change the public-health calculus immediately).

Risk framing — what the WHO and CDC actually say

"This is not the start of a COVID pandemic." — WHO, Maria Van Kerkhove. Risk to the American public and travelers remains extremely low.

Other emerging-threat radar (background)

Beyond Andes, infectious-disease experts in 2026 are watching: chikungunya (vector-driven expansion), measles (under-vaccination resurgence in multiple regions), and HIV (poised for a resurgence as international-aid disruptions reduce access to effective treatments). None has the "novel-spreading-pathogen" profile of Andes right now.

What it means: The cluster has not grown in three weeks; that is the good news. The bad news is that the 42-day incubation window won't close until mid-June, the ship hasn't docked yet, and the Andes virus's hallmark (the only human-to-human-transmissible hantavirus) means a single secondary case among repatriated-passenger contacts would re-open the entire risk picture. 6 Any new thing in the science world (critical) — this week's curated stack

Five threads of science output this week that genuinely move the needle, ordered by tractable importance.

(a) Migdal effect, observed at last

Researchers at the University of the Chinese Academy of Sciences reported the first direct experimental observation of the Migdal effect — a quantum process in which a recoiling atomic nucleus ejects an electron. Predicted by Migdal in 1939. Confirmation unlocks new approaches to searches for light dark matter by giving direct-detection experiments a calibrated signal channel they could only previously model.

(b) JWST COSMOS-Web cosmic-web map

NASA's James Webb Space Telescope published the sharpest cosmic-web map ever — tracing 164,000+ galaxies back to ~1B years after the Big Bang. The cosmic-web is the enormous hidden filamentary structure that connects galaxies; this is the clearest empirical handle yet on how that structure formed in the early universe.

(c) Senolytic aptamers — Mayo Clinic

Mayo Clinic reported (May 15) that synthetic-DNA aptamers can selectively bind senescent "zombie cells". Senescent cells accumulate with aging and drive inflammation; selectively eliminating them is one of the most promising age-related-disease pathways. The aptamer approach gets around the toxicity issues of small-molecule senolytics. Trial-ready binding chemistry, not a drug yet, but the most concrete senolytic step of 2026 so far.

(d) Voronoi diagrams in Chinese money plant leaves

Plant biologists discovered that the venation pattern in Chinese money plant leaves follows a naturally occurring Voronoi diagram — the same partitioning algorithm used in city planning, computer networks and computational geometry. Suggests an underlying optimisation rule in leaf morphogenesis that may be transferable to other tissue-structure problems.

(e) Oxford "quadsqueezing"; Tokyo positronium interference; stroke-recovery protein target

  • Oxford physicists: first-ever demonstration of quadsqueezing — a new powerful way to control quantum systems, with applications across precision measurement and quantum sensing.
  • Tokyo University of Science (May 13): first observation of positronium-wave interference — a step toward antimatter interferometry.
  • Institute of Science Tokyo, Prof. Takashi Shichita's team (May 14): identified a protein target capable of extending post-stroke motor-recovery beyond the ~2-month plateau. Most concrete neurology pharmacology result of the month.

Honorable mention — atmosphere & climate

A paper this week shows CO₂ rapidly cooling the upper atmosphere even as surface heats — direct implications for satellite drag and re-entry orbit predictions in LEO. Marine biology: Western Australia deep-canyon survey reports giant-squid evidence + new species. NASA Backyard Worlds project doubled the catalogue of known brown dwarfs to >6,000.

What it means: The pattern of the week is old predictions finally observed — Migdal (1939), positronium interference, Voronoi-in-nature. That's healthy: it means apparatus and detection sensitivity have caught up with theory. The Mayo aptamer result and the stroke-recovery protein result are the two that could reach patients within a near-term horizon; everything else is foundational.
