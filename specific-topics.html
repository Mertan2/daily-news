🕊️ Reading the Iran "deal" — why markets moved before any signature

Thursday's reversal is the story of the week, but it comes with a trap: there is no signed agreement yet. What Trump announced is a framework — a 60-day memorandum of understanding he himself called "a little conceptual" — and the parties most central to it are not all on board. Iran's foreign ministry called a finalized deal "speculation," and Netanyahu said Israel is not a party. Yet oil fell ~4% and equities ripped. Why?

Why it matters

  • markets price probabilitiesAn oil price isn't a verdict on whether the deal is signed — it's a bet on the odds and timing of more supply. The mere shift from "second day of strikes" to "signing this weekend" lowered the probability of a prolonged Hormuz closure, and that expected-value change is enough to move crude immediately. The market is front-running the supply, not confirming it.
  • the terms, decodedThe draft would extend the ceasefire (incl. Lebanon), reopen Hormuz toll-free, lift the US port blockade, grant sanctions waivers so Iran can sell oil again, and require Iran to clear mines within 30 days. Crucially, the hardest issue — Iran's enriched-uranium stockpile and nuclear program — is deferred to a second stage, with only a pledge not to build a weapon. Deferral is what made a fast framework possible; it's also the seam where it could tear.
  • the three veto pointsThree things can still break it: (1) Iran's Supreme Leader withholding final approval; (2) Israel — not a signatory — continuing strikes on Hezbollah, which is what reignited the war in the first place; and (3) the physical reality of Hormuz, where the US still downed two Iranian drones aimed at commercial ships even as the deal was announced. A framework can be unwound by any one of them.

The tell to watch

Watch three signals in order: an actual signature (Trump floats this weekend in Europe), the start of mine-clearing and a measurable rise in Hormuz transits, and Brent. If crude keeps sliding toward the $70s, the market believes it; a snap-back above $95 means a veto point fired. The inflation math from earlier this week (4.2% CPI, 6.5% PPI) reverses only if the oil decline sticks.

Plain-English glossary — MOU (memorandum of understanding): a written statement of intent that frames a deal but is not necessarily binding until formalized. Sanctions waiver: a temporary government exemption letting otherwise-blocked transactions (here, Iranian oil sales) proceed. "Pricing in": when asset prices move on the expectation of an event before it actually happens.

The risk is concentration: a company this size, this tied to one person, adds a new single-name swing factor to the indices.

Over-allotment ("greenshoe"): an option letting underwriters sell up to ~15% extra shares to meet demand and stabilize the price. Price discovery: the opening auction that sets the first public trade — for hot IPOs it can run hours after the 9:30 bell. Oversubscribed: more buy orders than shares available, a sign of strong demand (and a setup for a pop).

🏦 The rate-hike cluster — four central banks, one energy shock, and the carry trade

  • TCMB · doneHeld at 37% — but the signal was hawkish: the bank raised its end-2026 inflation forecast to 26% (from 18%) on the energy shock, while keeping policy restrictive.
  • BOJ · June 15–16The one to watch: a hike to 1.0% from 0.75% is now an 80–96% market call — the first 1% rate since 1995. After decades near zero, this is a regime change, not a tweak.
  • Fed · June 16–17Expected to hold, but a 6.5% PPI plus a strong labor market has the market pricing essentially no 2026 cuts and rising hike odds — even as falling oil now pulls the other way.

The carry-trade risk, explained

For years, investors borrowed cheap yen at near-zero rates and parked the money in higher-yielding assets abroad — the yen carry trade. When the BOJ raises rates and the yen strengthens, that trade gets more expensive and less profitable, so positions unwind: traders sell foreign assets and buy back yen, which can ripple through global markets fast (as it did in August 2024). A hawkish BOJ surprise next week is the single cleanest channel by which a Tokyo decision could jolt New York and London.

Carry trade: borrowing in a low-rate currency to invest in a higher-rate one, pocketing the spread — profitable until the funding currency rises or rates converge. Basis points (bp): hundredths of a percent; 25bp = 0.25%.

🇹🇷 The CHP convention war — the math behind the meltdown

Türkiye's main opposition party tore itself in two on June 11, and the drama is best understood as a fight over procedural arithmetic: who can force an extraordinary party convention, and who can be expelled before one happens.

  • the resignations28 of the ~57 Party Council members aligned with Özgür Özel resigned together. That wasn't a protest — it was a tactic. Dropping the council below its two-thirds quorum can trigger the rule that mandates an extraordinary convention within 45 days, wresting the timetable away from Kılıçdaroğlu.
  • the counter-moveIn parallel, Kılıçdaroğlu's executive board referred nine deputies to the High Discipline Board seeking expulsion over alleged 2023 delegate vote-buying. Expulsions thin the rival camp's delegate strength before any convention — but spokesman Müslim Sarı stressed that Özel and the detained İmamoğlu are not on the expulsion list, keeping the fight one level below the top.
  • the deadline that frames it allBoth sides are racing a July 25 marker; Özel calls a convention before then "unavoidable." Whoever controls the convention's timing and delegate roster controls the leadership — and, by extension, the party's 2028 presidential nomination, which is why İmamoğlu (held in Silivri since March 2025) is backing Özel's camp.

Why it matters beyond party politics: the CHP is the main check on Erdoğan, and a self-inflicted split weakens the opposition just as Türkiye absorbs an energy-driven inflation shock (CBRT now sees 26% by year-end). Watch the courts — Özel's side says it will sue to overturn the expulsions — and whether the 45-day convention clock actually starts.

Party Council (PM / Parti Meclisi): the CHP's top decision-making body between conventions. Extraordinary convention: an off-cycle congress that can re-elect or replace the leader. Quorum: the minimum share of members required for a body to act — here, falling below two-thirds is itself the trigger.
