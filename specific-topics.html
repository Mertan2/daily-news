🛢️ Anatomy of the US–Iran deal

what just happenedAfter two days of US strikes (June 9–10), a closed Strait of Hormuz and a spike in global oil, the war ended on paper this weekend. Trump declared the agreement "now complete," ordered the US naval blockade lifted and authorized a toll-free reopening of Hormuz. The mediation was run by Pakistan's PM Shehbaz Sharif, who announced an "immediate and permanent termination of military operations on all fronts, including Lebanon."

The reported terms

  • Hormuz reopens immediately, toll-free, with the US blockade lifted in parallel.
  • ~$25 billion of Iran's frozen assets released, some via direct cash transfers.
  • Oil sanctions waived; no new sanctions until a final, comprehensive deal.
  • Iran reaffirms it will not produce nuclear weapons — but the full nuclear file (enrichment, stockpiles) is deferred to a second stage.
  • A formal signing ceremony is set for June 19 in Switzerland.

Why it could still break

Three veto points remain live. Israel insists it is "not a party" and kept up strikes in Lebanon over the weekend that directly threaten the truce. Iran's final sign-off isn't locked until June 19. And any physical incident in Hormuz — a mine, a drone — could unravel the choreography. The market's read is optimistic for now: Brent fell ~5% to ~$81, an eight-week low.

Why it matters beyond the Gulf: the energy shock drove the recent US inflation spike (CPI 4.2%, PPI 6.5%). A durable reopening of Hormuz is the single biggest disinflationary lever heading into the Fed's June 16–17 meeting — which is exactly why oil, not the communiqués, is the signal to watch.

🦠 The Ebola Bundibugyo emergency

where it standsThe Central Africa outbreak caused by the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola keeps climbing. As of June 10, the DRC reported 676 confirmed cases and 136 deaths, with Ituri province the epicentre (629 cases across 19 health zones) and a smaller cluster in Uganda. The WHO declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern on May 17.

Why this one is hard

  • No approved vaccine or treatment exists for the Bundibugyo strain specifically — the licensed Ebola vaccines target the Zaire species, leaving responders without their usual frontline tool.
  • Rapid early-June growth (from ~378 cases on June 2 to 676 by June 10) points to active community transmission that contact tracing hasn't yet contained.
  • Ituri's security and access challenges complicate case-finding and burials, the classic drivers of Ebola spread.

The international picture

The US CDC has assessed the risk to the US population as low, and a continental response plan is underway. The key metrics to watch this week: whether the daily case curve in Ituri bends, the funding behind the response, and any first case outside the DRC/Uganda corridor.
