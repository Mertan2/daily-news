🕊️ The disowned deal — why oil moved on a framework even Trump is now contradicting

Thursday's reversal looked like the end of the war; by Saturday it looks like the start of a negotiation about the negotiation. Trump spent Friday publicly rejecting the 14-point terms that Iran's Mehr News Agency had published, insisting they had "NOTHING to do with the terms that were agreed to, in writing." Tehran says nothing is final. Israel says it isn't even in the room. And yet Brent fell to a near two-month low. How can a contradicted, unsigned framework move a global commodity?

Why it matters

  • price = probability × supplyAn oil price is not a verdict on whether a deal is signed — it's the market's running estimate of how much crude will flow, and when. The shift from "a second day of US strikes" to "a signing this weekend" lowered the odds of a prolonged Hormuz closure. Even discounted for the chance it falls apart, that expected-value change is enough to drag crude lower immediately. The market is front-running barrels, not certifying a treaty.
  • the disagreement is the signalThe fact that Washington and Tehran are fighting over the text in public tells you the gap is now about terms (frozen funds, blockade sequencing, sanctions wording) rather than whether to talk at all. That's a narrower, more tractable fight than active war — which is why traders treated Trump's denial as noise around a still-improving baseline, not a collapse.
  • the three things still left to clearThe draft would extend the ceasefire (incl. Lebanon), reopen Hormuz toll-free in 30 days, lift the blockade, waive oil sanctions, and defer the nuclear file. Iran wants (1) ~half its frozen assets released and (2) the blockade ended before final talks; the US wants the mine-clearing and a no-weapon pledge locked first. (3) Israel, outside the deal, can still reignite the war by striking Hezbollah — the exact trigger that broke the April ceasefire.

The tell to watch

Three signals, in order: an actual signature (Geneva is the floated venue, with Vance attending), the start of mine-clearing and a measurable rise in Hormuz transits, and Brent itself. If crude keeps grinding toward the $70s, the market believes it; a snap-back above $95 means a veto point fired. The week's inflation math (4.2% CPI, 6.5% PPI) only reverses if the oil decline sticks for weeks, not days.

Plain-English glossary — MOU (memorandum of understanding): a written statement of intent that frames a deal but isn't necessarily binding until formalized. Sanctions waiver: a temporary government exemption letting otherwise-blocked transactions (here, Iranian oil sales) proceed. "Pricing in": when asset prices move on the expectation of an event before it happens — and unwind if it doesn't.

Plain-English glossary — S-1: the registration document a US company files with the SEC before going public; a confidential S-1 lets the SEC review it before the financials become public. Post-money valuation: a company's implied worth right after a funding round, including the new cash.

Why it matters

  • two banks, one shock, opposite movesThe Fed is expected to hold while signaling a possible bias shift from easing toward neutral or tightening — an awkward posture with inflation surging (4.2% CPI / 6.5% PPI) but the labor market still healthy. The BOJ is leaning the other way, toward its first hike to 1.0% in nearly 30 years, to fight imported inflation. Same energy shock, mirror-image responses.
  • the carry-trade trip-wireA hawkish BOJ into a weak yen (~¥160) is the classic setup for an unwind of the yen carry trade — investors who borrowed cheap yen to buy higher-yielding assets get squeezed when Japanese rates rise and the yen strengthens. That's the cross-asset risk to watch if Tokyo surprises, especially with markets already jumpy from the oil round-trip.
  • a politics-vs-mandate collisionPM Takaichi's entire economic strategy rests on a weak yen — which depends on the BOJ not hiking. A rate increase cuts directly against the government's bet, turning a technical decision into a political one. Ueda's absence means a hawkish move (or a dovish hold) lands without the governor's voice to frame it.

The tell to watch

Watch USD/JPY around the BOJ decision (a sharp move below ¥155 signals a carry unwind), the Fed's updated dot plot Wednesday for whether "tightening" enters the conversation, and the 10-year Treasury (~4.5%) as the bond market's verdict on whether the oil-driven inflation spike is peaking or persisting.

Plain-English glossary — carry trade: borrowing in a low-rate currency (yen) to invest in higher-yielding assets; profitable until the funding currency rises. Dot plot: the chart of individual Fed officials' rate forecasts, released quarterly. Policy bias: whether a central bank is leaning toward cutting (easing), holding (neutral), or raising (tightening) rates next.

🇹🇷 "Absolute nullity" — the law behind Türkiye's opposition crisis

Türkiye's main opposition party, the CHP, is paralyzed by a legal doctrine most people had never heard of a month ago: mutlak butlan, or "absolute nullity." On May 21, the Ankara Regional Court of Appeals annulled the CHP's 38th Ordinary Convention (held Nov 2023) — the very gathering at which Özgür Özel defeated longtime leader Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu. The ruling didn't just void a vote; it potentially rewinds the party's leadership by two and a half years.

Why it matters

  • what "absolute nullity" meansIn Turkish civil law, mutlak butlan treats an act as void from the start — as if it never legally happened — rather than merely voidable going forward. Applied to a party convention, the theory is that procedural defects (here, alleged irregularities/vote-buying claims) make Özel's election a legal non-event, which is why the court could reinstate Kılıçdaroğlu rather than simply order a re-run.
  • two readings of "what next"The doctrine creates a standoff. Kılıçdaroğlu's camp argues the nullity reinstates him and that no new convention can be held until a definitive higher-court ruling settles the question. Özel's camp argues the only legitimate fix is an extraordinary convention "as a matter of urgency" to re-establish a mandate from the membership. This week that produced rival parliamentary group meetings, two podiums, and a scuffle outside the Assembly — a party visibly operating as two parties.
  • the stakes beyond the CHPThis is unfolding while İmamoğlu — the opposition's strongest 2028 presidential prospect — is in detention and backing Özel, and amid a broader protest movement. Whoever controls the CHP machinery controls candidate selection and the party's mandate heading into 2028. The currency (lira ~46/$) and the TCMB's hold at 37% are the macro backdrop the fight plays out against.

The tell to watch

The legal track is decisive: watch for a ruling from the Court of Cassation (Türkiye's top civil court) that either confirms or overturns the nullity, any move to actually schedule an extraordinary convention, and how İmamoğlu messages from detention. The lira is the real-time gauge of whether markets read the crisis as contained or spreading.

Plain-English glossary — mutlak butlan / absolute nullity: a legal act so flawed it's treated as never having existed, not just cancelled forward. Extraordinary convention: an unscheduled party congress called to resolve a crisis (vs. a routine "ordinary" one). Court of Cassation: Türkiye's highest court of appeal for civil and criminal cases.
