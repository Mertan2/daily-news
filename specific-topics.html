Why the reaction was so violent

The AI trade of the last two years rested on a simple assumption: demand for accelerators and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) would compound as models scaled. Korea's market is unusually exposed to that bet — Samsung and SK Hynix dominate the index to the point that Goldman Sachs estimates each one-percentage-point shift in their combined weight can trigger ~$2 billion of foreign selling. So an efficiency story that questions unit demand doesn't just ding two stocks; it moves the whole tape.

The counter-argument: Jevons, not doom

Efficiency has historically increased total consumption of a resource, not shrunk it — the Jevons paradox. Cheaper inference could unlock far more AI usage (agents, always-on assistants, real-time video), soaking up any freed capacity and then some. The same week also delivered the OpenAI–Nvidia 10-gigawatt partnership (Nvidia investing up to $100B, first GW on the Vera Rubin platform in H2 2026) — hardly the behavior of a company expecting to need fewer chips. And OpenAI's own custom inference chip, "Jalapeño" (built with Broadcom, pitched on performance-per-watt), suggests the constraint it's optimizing is cost-at-scale, not scarcity.

What to verify before trusting the move

  • SourcingThe "~50%" figure is a reported claim, not a published, peer-reviewed benchmark. A one-day 8% index crash on a single unverified number is itself a signal of how crowded the trade had become.
  • HBM pricingWatch spot and contract HBM prices in the coming weeks — the cleanest read on whether real demand is softening or traders are just de-risking.
  • Capex guidance~$650B of 2026 hyperscaler capex is the number under the whole edifice. If any major buyer trims guidance citing efficiency, the selloff was early, not wrong.

So what: For two years "more compute" was the only trade. The market just got its first serious scare that the equation could flip — and discovered how few names it was really leaning on. Whether this is a top or a headfake hinges on one thing: does cheaper AI get used more, or just built less?

The question no one has rehearsed

Khamenei led Iran for over three decades. The ceasefire that ended the fighting was built around his Iran; whether it survives contact with a new leadership — and whether that leadership wants the nuclear talks to resume at all — is now the single most important variable in the region.

So what: The June war didn't just pause over a document; it removed the man who had defined Iranian strategy for a generation. The week's ceasefire question is settled for now. The harder one — what kind of Iran emerges from this funeral — is just beginning, and every barrel of oil and every stalled negotiation hangs on it.
