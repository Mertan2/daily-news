2 Neurological / neuromorphic computers — BCI is a two-horse race; neuromorphic substrate is moving

Paradromics' May 14 Connexus implant — the anchor result holds

The Connexus BCI in-human implant at the University of Michigan on May 14 is still the only single event this month that genuinely repositions the BCI race. The procedure: a dime-sized 421-microwire-electrode array placed in the temporal lobe of an epilepsy patient already in scheduled neurosurgery, by Dr. Oren Sagher with research lead Dr. Matthew Willsey. ~20-minute implant-plus-explant cycle. Neural recording confirmed at single-neuron resolution.

  • Connexus is fully implantable; targets restoration of speech/cursor control for ALS, paralysis, locked-in syndrome.
  • Published sheep-trial data: ~200 bps throughput — roughly 25× Neuralink's 8-bps cursor record.
  • Mechanism: EpiPen-like inserter vs Neuralink's robotic insertion. Cheaper to scale, easier to credential.
  • FDA approval already in hand for a long-term speech-restoration trial; two volunteers with neurological speech loss implanted earlier this year.
  • This week's announcement: Paradromics' high-bandwidth Connexus successfully enabled a locked-in-syndrome participant to communicate at speeds approaching natural speech — first credible "near-speech-rate" demonstration in a chronically implanted human.

Neuralink — second-gen FDA approval and 12 implanted patients

  • Neuralink received FDA approval for its second-generation implant system this quarter; successfully restored movement and communication capabilities to multiple patients with paralysis.
  • April disclosure: 45 enrolled across three continents.
  • ~85% of spinal-injury participants achieving pick-and-place at ≥150% of non-injured baselines.
  • May 7: new surgical robot capable of reaching virtually any brain region; threads now insert through dura (no removal). Targets Parkinson's, epilepsy, sensory disorders.
  • 12 patients currently implanted; Musk reiterates "high-volume production" target for 2026.
  • Patients now using the N1 daily to play video games, browse the web, and control external hardware.

The neuromorphic substrate — chips that compute like brains

  • Cambridge hafnium-oxide memristor (May 13, Science Advances): Sr/Ti-doped HfO₂ with engineered p-n junctions yields ~1,000,000× lower switching current than conventional memristors and hundreds of stable conductance levels. ~70% AI energy reduction vs conventional GPUs running equivalent workloads. Architectural shift: from filament-switching to interface-based devices — the architecture analog-AI computing has been waiting on. Caveat: 700 °C fab temperature exceeds standard semi tolerance — production-scale is not next quarter.
  • Penn light-matter computing (May 18, ScienceDaily): hybrid light-matter particle that could speed up AI computing while using far less energy; pathway to replace some electronic computing processes with ultra-efficient light-based technology.
  • KTH chip for dissipation: enables simulation of how quantum systems behave when they lose energy or information to their surroundings — a long-missing tool for analog-AI noise modelling.
  • Biological Interface System to Cortex (BISC): single CMOS chip thinned to 50 µm, tens of thousands of electrodes, 1,000+ recording channels, 16,000+ stimulation channels — third platform in serious circulation alongside Neuralink and Connexus.
What it means: The "Neuralink is alone" narrative is over. Paradromics has now demonstrated (a) a chronically implanted device in a human, (b) near-natural-speech communication, and (c) a procurement-friendly insertion path — at ~25× the data rate of Neuralink's last public cursor benchmark. Both teams now face the same gating step: a multi-year FDA path through a chronically implanted restorative device. On the substrate side, Cambridge's HfO₂ result is a "different physics" claim, not an incremental optimisation — but ~24 months from any fab. If both lines of work hold, by 2028 we should have BCI-class restorative devices on commodity neuromorphic silicon, and the energy-per-inference floor falls by an order of magnitude.

3 Quantum computing — Jiuzhang 4.0 is the biggest single jump in optical quantum since 2019

The new headline: Jiuzhang 4.0 (Nature, May 13)

USTC team (Pan Jianwei et al) published in Nature May 13: Jiuzhang 4.0 manipulated and detected 3,050 photons on Gaussian boson sampling — a leap from the 255 photons of Jiuzhang 3.0. Reported ~10⁵⁴× speedup over the world's fastest supercomputer on the GBS task. Pathway opens toward trillion-qubit-mode three-dimensional cluster states for fault-tolerant optical quantum computing. This is the densest single result the optical-quantum field has produced since Google's October 2019 supremacy paper.

Photonic W-state — Kyoto + Hiroshima (May 13)

  • First experimental W-state entangled measurement using 3 photons via cyclic-shift symmetry through a photonic quantum-Fourier-transform circuit.
  • Resolves a 25-year open problem (GHZ-state measurement long solved; W-state was not).
  • Generalises to any number of photons; enables new teleportation/communication protocols.
  • Corresponding author: Shigeki Takeuchi.

Classical simulation of quantum — Jülich + NVIDIA (May 11)

  • JUPITER exascale + NVIDIA-upgraded JUQCS-50 simulated a full 50-qubit universal quantum computer (~2 PB memory).
  • 11.4× speedup over the 2019 K-computer 48-qubit record.
  • Reset the classical benchmark every near-term quantum-advantage claim has to clear.

Neutral-atom — ETH Zurich (Nature)

  • Geometric-phase swap gates on 17,000 neutral-atom qubits at 99.91% fidelity; robust against laser noise.
  • Esslinger group at IQE.

Trapped-ion — Oxford (May 1, Nature Physics)

  • First "quadsqueezing" — fourth-order quantum squeezing in a single trapped ion via two simultaneous control forces.
  • >100× faster than conventional approaches.
  • Applications: LIGO-class quantum sensing, simulation.

Superconducting + error suppression — Q-CTRL × IBM (Think 2026, May 4)

  • 3,000× classical speedup on a 120-qubit Fermi-Hubbard simulation (100 hours classical → 2 minutes quantum) via runtime error suppression on IBM Quantum Platform.
  • Q-CTRL CEO Biercuk: practical quantum advantage "already here."

Photonic teleportation + chip-noise simulation

  • 270-meter photon teleportation (late April, ScienceDaily) — record distance for a deterministic teleportation protocol in atmospheric channel; positions photonic quantum networking as a viable medium-range communication primitive.
  • KTH chip for simulating quantum-system dissipation — enables study of imperfections without exotic isolation; opens a new pathway for noisy-intermediate-scale device design.

Infrastructure — Fujitsu × Institute of Science Tokyo (May 15)

  • Launched the "Fujitsu Quantum and HPC Infrastructure Collaborative Research Cluster"; quantum hub at Ookayama, HPC hub at Yokohama.
  • Full-cycle scope: qubit chip design, fabrication, control/calibration with AI-driven gate calibration.
  • Explicit talent pipeline — Japan's bet that the next decade of quantum is won on integration not qubit count. First operational tests now passed (this week).
What it means: Jiuzhang 4.0 alone is the biggest single jump in optical quantum computing since 2019. Combined with the Q-CTRL/IBM 3000× benchmark on a 120-qubit Fermi-Hubbard simulation and the Jülich classical-simulation refresh, "useful quantum advantage" is now a benchmark that classical supercomputers can no longer chase in a useful timeframe on certain problem classes. The frame has shifted from "qubit count" to "useful, error-robust gates at scale", and the field is delivering. Three of the May results — Kyoto's photonic measurement, ETH's neutral-atom fidelity, Oxford's quadsqueezing — are physics results, not engineering optimisations; each opens an experimental category that wasn't possible two weeks ago.

6 Any new thing in the science world (critical) — muon anomaly closes, anti-aging protein lands, string-theory clue returns

Physics — the muon-g–2 anomaly was likely a calculation error

  • A supercomputer recalculation reported this month suggests the decades-long muon-g–2 anomaly was a calculation error, not a hidden new force. After years of "fifth-force" speculation, the apparent discrepancy between Fermilab's measurement and Standard Model prediction is now most likely a Standard Model bookkeeping miss.
  • Critical because: a generation of "physics beyond the Standard Model" research used this anomaly as a target. If the result holds up through peer review, multiple beyond-the-Standard-Model pathways need new motivation.

Physics — string theory gets a clue that may be testable

  • Separately reported mid-May: a research team identified a mathematical clue suggesting string theory could be more than a mathematical fantasy — observable consequences possibly extractable.
  • Final-state TBD over the next year; the field is treating this with cautious optimism.

Aging biology — a protein that brakes inflammation, in mice

  • Mid-May research (ScienceDaily synthesis): scientists identified a protein that "puts the brakes" on chronic inflammation linked to aging. Older mice with boosted protein levels were stronger, more energetic, and had healthier bones.
  • Pairs with the Mayo Clinic senescence-tag aptamer result (Aging Cell May 15): synthetic DNA aptamers selectively tag senescent cells in mouse tissue from 100T+ random sequences — finally clears the detection bottleneck that has stalled 30+ senolytic trials.
  • Together, these two results may push senolytic / anti-inflammatory aging research into a new clinical phase by 2027.

Biology / environmental health — kimchi probiotic accelerates microplastic clearance

  • South Korean group reported May 18 that a probiotic bacterium isolated from kimchi appears to help the body flush tiny plastic particles before they build up in organs (mouse model).
  • First credible "you can already buy this" intervention against the chronic-microplastic-load problem — though human-trial data is years out.

Plant biology — Voronoi venation, first in vivo demonstration

  • Pilea peperomioides (Chinese money plant) leaf veins form a Voronoi diagram around hydathode pores (34 leaves, 6 plants; Nature Communications May 12) — first in-vivo demonstration with both edges and centres functional.
  • The crispest case of a real biological structure obeying a textbook mathematical model — leaf hydraulics now traceable cell-by-cell.

Astrobiology — statistical "biosignature" pattern in amino acids

  • Nature Astronomy (mid-May): amino acids in living systems carry a statistical signature — both more varied and more evenly distributed than amino acids produced abiotically.
  • Practical upshot: the answer to "is this alien biology?" may sit not in any single molecule but in the pattern across the molecules.
  • Implications for upcoming Europa / Enceladus / Mars sample analyses.

Materials / matter physics — exotic forms of matter

  • Early-May ScienceDaily compilation: scientists created "exotic new forms of matter that shouldn't exist" — driven-dissipative phases that fall outside equilibrium classifications. Material substrate for next-generation sensors and computation.
