3. Quantum Computing

A 3,000× speedup on a real materials problem; the first verifiable quantum advantage published in Nature; and a fault-tolerance ladder now measured in months, not decades.

2026 is the year quantum stopped being only a roadmap. The Q-CTRL × IBM materials-science demo on a 120-qubit machine — done in two minutes instead of 100 classical hours, within 1% accuracy — is the first credible "useful quantum advantage" in an industrially relevant workload. Google's Willow chip went one step further by publishing a verifiable quantum advantage in Nature: a Quantum Echoes algorithm 13,000× faster than the best classical alternative and reproducible on hardware. And Germany's new exascale supercomputer JUPITER fully simulated a 50-qubit quantum computer classically — the wall that future "supremacy" claims will have to clear.

4. New Spreading Virus — MV Hondius hantavirus cluster

Eleven cases (9 confirmed/2 probable in newer counts; 7+2 in the latest WHO DON), three deaths, one ship, six destination countries. Andes virus — the only hantavirus that spreads human-to-human.

On May 2, the WHO was notified of severe respiratory illness on the MV Hondius, a Dutch cruise ship that left Ushuaia, Argentina on April 1. The pathogen was identified as Andes hantavirus — the one hantavirus known to spread person-to-person, and even then almost only with close, sustained contact. The Spanish Ministry of Health let the ship dock at Tenerife on May 10; 147 people including additional medical resources were on board; passengers were repatriated to six European countries and Canada. As of May 11, WHO's DON600 lists 7 confirmed + 2 probable cases and 3 deaths (2 attributable to hantavirus, 1 still under investigation). Several outlets report a higher running case count of 11 once probable cases are folded in.

Pathogen
Andes virus (Bunyavirales / Hantaviridae). Reservoir: long-tailed pygmy rice rat in southern South America. Only hantavirus with documented human-to-human transmission, typically requiring prolonged close contact, possibly airborne in very close proximity.
Index case
Most probably acquired infection ashore in Argentina during pre-cruise activities; on-board cases linked epidemiologically and temporally to case 1's illness.
Mortality risk
Hantavirus Cardiopulmonary Syndrome (HCPS): historical case-fatality 30–50% in South America. No specific antiviral; supportive ICU care is the standard.
Risk to general public
Low. CDC and ECDC concur. No community spread documented; contact tracing focused on cabin-mates, medical staff, and close family.

6. Any new thing in science (critical)

Four wires to watch: a world-first in antimatter physics, a wildfire trajectory shattering records, organic chemistry on Mars, and a quietly significant pivot in obesity medicine.

(a) First quantum interference observed in a positronium beam

A Tokyo University of Science team (Nagashima, Nagata, Mikami) published the first observation of matter-wave diffraction in a positronium beam in Nature Communications. Energies up to 3.3 keV in ultra-high vacuum on a graphene grating. Why it matters: positronium (an electron + positron bound state) is a clean two-body atom whose wave behaviour opens an unprecedented experimental window onto antimatter — including, eventually, a direct measurement of gravity's effect on antimatter, never directly tested. Nature Comms · TUS · SciTechDaily

(b) Global wildfires already 20%+ above the prior full-year record

The Global Wildfire Information System reports 163M hectares burned globally between January 1 and the first week of May — >20% above the previous record since 2012 tracking began. Sahel (west Africa, Sudan, South Sudan) and Asia (+~40% over the prior peak) lead; the U.S. and Australia are also unusually high. World Weather Attribution warned Tuesday that if a strong El Niño develops, full-year 2026 could be the worst ever recorded. Down To Earth · Malay Mail · Channels

(c) Curiosity rover finds DNA-precursor-like organics on Mars

NASA Curiosity's analysis of ancient clay-rich rocks at Gale Crater turned up a surprising variety of organic molecules including some resembling DNA building blocks — preserved in materials that once held water. Not proof of life, but the strongest signal yet that early Mars was biologically more promising than thought. The team's separate "Atacama-rock" drill incident (a rock came out of the ground stuck to the drill) was resolved last week. Psyche spacecraft does a Mars gravity-assist flyby Friday May 15 at 2,800 mi. ScienceDaily — Organics · ScienceDaily — Atacama

(d) ATTAIN-MAINTAIN — oral GLP-1 holds most of the weight loss

A Weill Cornell-led trial published yesterday in Nature Medicine showed that switching from an injectable GLP-1 (tirzepatide) to once-daily oral orforglipron preserved most — but not all — of patients' weight loss over the maintenance phase. Combined with NIH brain-circuit work showing small-molecule GLP-1s suppress hedonic feeding through a reward circuit, the field is rapidly moving toward cheaper, oral, all-day pharmacotherapy. (e) Fundamental constants and the existence of life

Queen Mary, London proposed that the universe's fundamental constants sit in a narrow band that permits liquids — including blood and the cytoplasm of cells — to flow at all. If they were slightly different, complex life could never have emerged. A new entry in the "fine-tuning" literature, but with falsifiable thermodynamic content. ScienceDaily · Huxley.media
