1 · The AI trade learns to tell the difference

This week produced one of the sharpest whipsaws in a major equity index in years, and the round trip is more informative than either leg.

The rule the market applied

Two weeks ago the market punished capital expenditure indiscriminately: Alphabet beat on revenue, raised capex guidance, and fell. That was a blunt instrument. This week it applied a sharper one — spending is fine if the revenue line moves with it.

  • Microsoft: huge capex, Azure accelerating to +43%. Rewarded with the largest single-day value gain by any stock on record.
  • Amazon: trailing-twelve-month property and equipment purchases of $169bn, up 64% — and AWS growth of 37%, its fastest in eighteen quarters, at a 39.4% operating margin. Rewarded.
  • Meta: raised the floor of the capex range without new revenue evidence. Punished 8%, on an EPS miss that was mostly one-time legal and severance items.
  • Apple: barely plays this game, beat both lines, and was still punished — for China demand and a guided gross-margin decline. Which shows the market is not simply pro-AI; it is pro-evidence.
The interesting disclosure nobody punished: Microsoft stated that roughly two-thirds of its capital expenditure goes to "short-lived assets, primarily CPUs and GPUs." That is a depreciation schedule measured in a handful of years against infrastructure being financed like it lasts decades. On a week when the market claimed to be scrutinising capex quality, this went through unchallenged.

Why Seoul moved four times as hard as anywhere else

Korea is the pure-play expression of this trade with no diversification to hide behind. Samsung and SK Hynix are memory; memory is the component with the tightest supply and the most exposed competitive flank. The Tuesday crash was not really about AI sentiment — it was about CXMT listing at a ~$489bn valuation and a report that China had begun mass-producing domestic immersion DUV lithography. The Friday rebound was not really about Korea either — it was Microsoft and Amazon proving the demand side is intact.

Underneath, the fundamentals never wobbled: SK Hynix reported record quarterly revenue on Wednesday at a 76% operating margin, holding roughly 56% of the HBM market by revenue.

Scale check. The Kospi closed Friday around 6,515 — still roughly 28% below the 9,000-plus it reached in June. A 17% fall followed by a 16.5% rise is not a round trip; it is a market with no settled price for memory, oscillating on each new data point.

What would actually resolve this

Three things, none of them sentiment: independent verification of yield, overlay accuracy and throughput on China's domestic DUV tool (currently a single reporting chain, with 2026 output targets around five machines); HBM4 pricing and shipment commentary through the next cycle; and whether the MATCH Act's 150-day clock on Japan and the Netherlands survives contact with two allied governments that would lose service revenue by complying.

2 · The pause that wasn't a ceasefire — and what survived it

Tuesday's briefing described a war "paused" for a third night, with Brent down 11% and Pakistan reportedly preparing to mediate. Three days later CENTCOM was running a two-hour barrage against dozens of IRGC targets. The pause lasted about four nights. Understanding why it collapsed matters more than the collapse itself, because the same structure will produce the same result next time.

There were two negotiations, not one

They were consistently reported as though they were the same thing. They were not:

  • The shipping track. Iranian and Omani deputy foreign ministers, meeting in Tehran, working on "common principles and operational mechanisms for managing safe shipping traffic" through Hormuz while respecting the sovereign rights of the two coastal states. Concrete, technical, bilateral between neighbours, and it is still running. Transits went from single digits to 14 on Wednesday.
  • The war track. Reported American expectations of Pakistani mediation, against Tehran stating flatly it had no negotiations with the United States — with its foreign ministry allowing only that "mediators may convey messages to us from the American side." There was no framework, no timetable, and no agreed agenda. There was a bombing halt.
Oil priced the first track as though it were the second. Brent fell from above $100 to $85.48 on the assumption that a shipping-management conversation in Tehran and a bombing halt added up to a settlement. When the IRGC fired on Jordan, the $5 came straight back.

What changed this week that hadn't before

Saudi Arabia became a participant. Riyadh joined US strikes on Iran-aligned groups. Through five months this war has been US-and-Israel against Iran, with the Gulf states hedging — hosting bases while keeping channels open. A Gulf state now has its own aircraft in the campaign, which changes what Iranian retaliation can target and makes the conflict harder to bound geographically.

Munitions became a stated constraint. Bloomberg's reporting frames Washington's choice as escalate-versus-restock, not escalate-versus-restraint. That is a materially different limit: political will can be summoned, inventory cannot.

The Jordan strike was aimed at a host, not just a force. Jordanian air defences intercepted five missiles. Iran is now firing into the territory of a US partner that has spent the war trying not to be one — testing whether basing arrangements survive being shot at.

The honest indicator

Not headlines, not oil. Hormuz transit counts. Under 10 vessels on Monday of last week against roughly 100 in a normal week; 14 on Wednesday. If that number keeps climbing while strikes continue, the shipping track is genuinely insulated from the war track and there is a functioning de-escalation mechanism, however narrow. If it collapses back to single digits, the two tracks were never separable and the oil market's next repricing will be violent in the other direction.

Contested throughout. Casualty figures, strike tallies and mediation claims come from combatant governments and single-outlet reporting. Iran's denial of negotiations and Washington's description of them cannot both be complete accounts.

Why the oil path is the whole story

Türkiye imports nearly all of its hydrocarbons. Brent's fall from $100 to $85 last week was, mechanically, the single most helpful development available to this policy stance — it eases the import bill, the currency pressure and the inflation path simultaneously, and it does so without the bank having to do anything. Brent's return to $90 this week withdraws part of that gift, and there is no scheduled instrument to respond with for six weeks.

The professional–household divergence is still open. The TCMB survey has professionals expecting 23.95% inflation twelve months out; Finance Minister Şimşek points to household twelve-month expectations falling 6.6 points in three months. Both cannot be a good guide to the September decision, and the bank has to pick one.

The political clock, running alongside

The Terörsüz Türkiye commission was seated today with 48 members. Its published allocation — AK Parti 21, CHP 10, MHP 4, DEM Parti 4, Yeni Yol 3, one each for DSP, HÜDA PAR and TİP — does not list Yeni Parti, the party Özgür Özel founded on 24 July with 91 departing CHP deputies and which reporting described as becoming the second-largest group in parliament. Either the seats were allocated on pre-split group sizes, or the defection did not translate into a registered parliamentary group at the scale reported.

This sweep could not determine which. Both the 91-deputy figure and the commission seat table come from mainstream Turkish reporting, and they are difficult to reconcile. Treat the seat table as an allocation document, not a map of current parliamentary strength, until a source resolves it.

Meanwhile the commission's substantive blockage has not moved all year: the PKK wants legislation addressing Öcalan's status; the government stays non-committal. And the detention of pro-government commentator Cem Küçük this week is the first high-profile case in a long sequence to run in that direction rather than toward the opposition — notable, but not yet interpretable.

4 · The Ebola outbreak nobody is pricing

Every other item on this page is being covered by hundreds of outlets. This one is being covered by the WHO bulletin, the ECDC page and MSF's field updates — and by the end of July it is described as the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record.

The facts

  • Declared 14 May 2026 in Ituri Province, Democratic Republic of the Congo.
  • Roughly 3,200 cases and 1,405 deaths as of 26 July — a case fatality ratio near 44%, in about ten weeks.
  • The pathogen is Bundibugyo ebolavirus, not Zaire ebolavirus.
  • Exported cases: a US citizen medically evacuated to Germany in May; a case confirmed in France on 24 June.
  • The UN has appointed veteran humanitarian coordinator Julien Harneis to lead the response.
The species distinction is the entire story. The licensed Ebola vaccines (Ervebo, Zabdeno/Mvabea) and the monoclonal therapeutics (Inmazeb, Ebanga) were developed and certified against Zaire ebolavirus. Against Bundibugyo, the countermeasure toolkit that turned the 2018–2020 Kivu epidemic from a catastrophe into a containable emergency does not straightforwardly apply. That is why the growth curve looks like a pre-2014 outbreak rather than a post-2019 one.

Why it belongs on a markets-and-politics page

Not because it is likely to move an index. Because the standard reason a story like this stays uncovered is that it is happening in Ituri rather than in a capital — and because two exported cases have already reached Europe within ten weeks of declaration. The relevant comparison is not to a typical Ebola outbreak; it is to the early weeks of an outbreak where the existing tools do not fit and the case count is compounding.

Figures are as-reported by WHO and national authorities, with the usual caveat that case ascertainment in an active outbreak lags reality in both directions — undercounting in inaccessible areas, over-attributing in surveillance-heavy ones. The 44% CFR is computed from the reported case and death totals above, not taken from a source.
