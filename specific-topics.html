Put together: the demand side of AI has never looked stronger (Micron), but the market is suddenly asking the hard questions — how long until the spending pays off, and what is it worth if money stays expensive?

The bull case is that these are the railroads and power utilities of the AI era — infrastructure whose returns compound for decades. The bear case is simpler: a lot of this is capital being spent today against revenue that is promised tomorrow, and any crack in the "tomorrow" timeline — like an IPO delay — forces a repricing.

The silicon: why Micron is the supercycle's cleanest gauge

If you want one number that tells you whether the AI build-out is real or hype, watch high-bandwidth memory (HBM). Every AI accelerator (Nvidia, AMD, custom silicon) needs stacks of it, and supply is tight. Micron is one of only three makers, so its order book is a near-real-time read on data-center demand.

  • The signal: guidance to $50bn next quarter and ~85% gross margins says hyperscalers are still buying everything that can be made — the physical build-out has not slowed.
  • The catch: memory is famously cyclical. Today's scarcity-driven margins invite tomorrow's capacity glut. The supercycle's skeptics watch HBM pricing the way oil traders watch rig counts.
  • The geopolitics: the same week, Beijing tightened rare-earth export controls on US firms and Washington reaffirmed that AI-chip license rules reach overseas subsidiaries — a reminder that the supply chain underneath all of this is a contested border, not a free market.

Friday's wobble: when "priced for perfection" meets a hawkish Fed

Here is the mechanism, step by step:

  • 1. Rates repriced up. May PCE ran hot (headline ~4.1%, core ~3.4% y/y), and the Warsh Fed has already signaled hikes, not cuts, for 2026. Higher rates lower the present value of distant cash flows — and AI valuations are almost entirely distant cash flows.
  • 2. A timeline crack. The OpenAI-delay report suggested the profit payoff is further away than hoped, just as the discount rate rose.
  • 3. Leverage unwound. Across Asia, leveraged AI bets were cut fast — Korea's Kospi hit a circuit breaker, Taiwan's Taiex fell ~3.6%, Hong Kong had its worst week since April 2025, and Tokyo gave back a record. US indexes ended roughly flat, cushioned by Micron's strength.

None of this breaks the long-term thesis. But it is the first time this cycle that the market has demanded evidence of profitability rather than taking it on faith — and that shift in posture is itself the news.
