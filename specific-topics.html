2 · Neurological computers (BCI & neuromorphic)

From request.md: "Neurological Computers"

First-everTsinghua's two-way adaptive BCI

Bioengineers at Tsinghua University published the world's first two-way adaptive brain-computer interface using a memristor-based adaptive neuromorphic decoder (Nature Electronics). "Two-way adaptive" is the key phrase — the brain and the decoder learn from each other in a closed loop rather than the user training a static decoder. Energy footprint is dramatically lower than digital decoders because of the memristor substrate, which is the neuromorphic equivalent of analog computing.

ScaleYale's NeuroScale — local-cluster sync replacing global clock

Yale researchers introduced NeuroScale, replacing the global synchronization clock of conventional neuromorphic chips with a decentralized scheme that synchronizes only local clusters of neurons and synapses that directly interact. The biological analogue: cortical microcircuits don't share a global timing signal. This is one of the cleanest pieces of work in 2026 on the path from chip-scale to system-scale neuromorphic computing.

HardwareBrain-inspired chips out of energy-efficiency necessity

As traditional CMOS chips reach their physical limits and AI inference demands more energy, neuromorphic computing is back in the conversation as a possible bridge. Cambridge HfO₂ memristor (May 13) delivered a ~70% AI-energy cut on representative workloads. The argument runs: if Moore's Law has fully tapered, then either silicon photonics, 3D stacking, or neuromorphic substrates are the only credible scale-out paths.

In-human BCINeuralink at scale; Paradromics densest dataset

Neuralink is in "high-volume production" mode per Musk — second-generation implant with FDA approval; speech-restoration FDA Breakthrough Device Designation; "almost entirely automated surgical flow" target for 2026. Last disclosed: 45 enrolled across 3 continents, 12 implanted; Grok integration brought closed-vocabulary word-error rate below 10%. Paradromics' Connexus (dime-sized 421-microwire array, single-neuron resolution) remains the densest in-human dataset — sheep showed ~200 bps, roughly 25× Neuralink's 8-bps cursor baseline. BISC third platform: 50-µm CMOS with 1,000+ recording and 16,000+ stim channels. BCI market ~$3.32B in 2026 per industry tracking.

Real neuronsArtificial neurons that physically reproduce biological behavior

Recent work has demonstrated artificial neurons that physically reproduce the way real neurons operate — unlike digital processors or earlier neuromorphic chips that only simulate brain activity via mathematical models. The implication is energy: a neuron-as-circuit-element does not need to compute through a multiply-accumulate pipeline to behave neuron-like.

Bottom line: The 2026 story is both directions converging: BCIs (Neuralink, Paradromics, BISC) scaling out in humans while neuromorphic substrates (Tsinghua, Yale NeuroScale, Cambridge HfO₂) scale out the chip side. The next-12-month milestone watch: Neuralink high-volume production proof, Paradromics first-in-human data, and the first cross-vendor BCI interoperability standard.

3 · Quantum computing

From request.md: "Quantum Computing"

Fault toleranceHarvard / Lukin lab pulls timeline forward

Researchers at the Mikhail Lukin lab at Harvard published a fault-tolerance advance significant enough that the field's median timeline for "some form" of a fault-tolerant quantum computer just moved from "end of next decade" to "end of this decade" — i.e., 2029-2030 instead of late 2030s. Fault tolerance is the make-or-break threshold for useful quantum: until you can correct calculation errors faster than they accrue, you cannot run a sustained quantum program. This is the Harvard team that has been publishing back-to-back logical-qubit breakthroughs on neutral-atom arrays.

SimulationJUPITER + NVIDIA 50-qubit universal simulation

The Jülich Supercomputing Centre + NVIDIA fully simulated a universal 50-qubit quantum computer on JUPITER — beats the 2019 48-qubit world record. Why simulate quantum on classical hardware? Validation: every new quantum chip needs a "ground truth" classical reference to score against. 50 qubits is the largest universal reference now available.

PhotonicKyoto + Hiroshima "W states" detected instantly

A photonic team from Kyoto and Hiroshima Universities designed a circuit that instantly detects three-photon "W states" — these are entangled multi-particle states central to quantum teleportation and quantum communication protocols. Demonstrated experimentally with three photons; stable and accurate. This resolves a ~25-year open instrumentation problem.

Optical & superconductingJiuzhang 4.0, ETH 17,000-atom, Oxford quadsqueezing

  • Jiuzhang 4.0 (Pan Jianwei lab, USTC, Nature): 3,050-photon optical quantum computer, claimed ~10⁵⁴× speedup over the world's fastest classical supercomputer on Gaussian boson sampling. Biggest single jump in optical quantum since 2019.
  • ETH Zürich: 17,000-atom array with 99.91%-fidelity gates (Nature) — the kind of fidelity number that finally crosses surface-code thresholds at scale.
  • Oxford "quadsqueezing" (Nature Physics): first-ever demonstration of quadsqueezing — an elusive fourth-order quantum effect, >100× faster than previous squeezing demonstrations. Useful for ultra-precise sensing.
  • Fujitsu × Sci-Tokyo: launched joint quantum-and-HPC cluster May 15 (operational tests now passed).
  • Q-CTRL × IBM: 3,000× speedup on a 120-qubit Fermi-Hubbard simulation (Think 2026 reveal).

SensingSub-zeptojoule sensor — quantum-grade thermometry

Researchers built an ultra-sensitive sensor capable of detecting below one zeptojoule of energy (10⁻²¹ J), relying on fragile superconducting materials that react to the slightest temperature change. Applications: dark-matter search, single-photon detection at telecom wavelengths.

Bottom line: Mid-2026 may be remembered as the quarter the quantum-computing timeline officially compressed. Every modality (photonic / neutral-atom / trapped-ion / superconducting) has now posted a 2026 headline result. Watchlist: Lukin lab's next logical-qubit paper, Jiuzhang 4.1 spec, ETH gate-fidelity follow-on, and Fujitsu × Sci-Tokyo's first published HPC-quantum benchmark.

Energy metabolismLeucine "supercharges" mitochondria

Researchers discovered that leucine — the branched-chain amino acid abundant in protein-rich foods — can supercharge mitochondria by protecting crucial energy-producing proteins inside cells. The mechanism is direct stabilization of inner-membrane respiratory proteins; the result is healthier cellular ATP output. Implications: muscle preservation, metabolic disease, aging research.

ChemistryLight-driven "housane" synthesis

Researchers developed a light-driven method for creating tiny, high-energy "housane" molecules, scaffolds prized for drug development and materials science. Housanes (bicyclo[2.1.0]pentanes) have been notoriously difficult to make cleanly; photochemical routes drop reaction step count significantly.

Biology / TaxonomyThe Himalayan pit viper is multiple species

The so-called "Himalayan pit viper," long catalogued as a single species, has been resolved into multiple distinct species based on genetic and morphological analysis. Antivenom and conservation implications: species-specific neutralisation profiles are now required.

An ultra-sensitive sensor built on fragile superconducting materials can now detect below one zeptojoule (10⁻²¹ J) of energy — the smallest unit of energy any laboratory instrument has been able to resolve. Direct relevance to dark-matter searches, single-photon telecom detection, gravitational-wave detector upgrades.
