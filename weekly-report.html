Weekly Report

Week of July 13 – 19, 2026

A running log of the week's major stories — updated each day. Latest revision: Friday, July 17. Where last week left off (July 6 – 12): the Gulf ceasefire shredded. Iran's IRGC fired on commercial ships in the Strait of Hormuz — including the Qatari LNG tanker Al-Rekayyat — and US Central Command answered by striking 80+ targets in Iran, including 60+ IRGC boats. Khamenei's seven-day state funeral closed with presumed successor Mojtaba still unseen, and nuclear talks stayed paused. The 36th NATO summit in Ankara produced a short declaration: Article 5 reaffirmed, a ~€70bn Ukraine pledge, the 5%-of-GDP target locked, and the F-35 door reopened for Türkiye — even as a pre-summit sweep detained 209 people. Markets whipsawed twice: the AI-chip rebound unwound violently on a scoop that DeepSeek is building its own inference chip (KOSPI −7.6% and circuit-broken). Russian barrages killed 22 in Ukraine against a Patriot shortage. This week: the war escalated toward Tehran, the AI trade broke again — this time on record earnings — and Britain acquired a prime minister without an election.

Daily Log

Monday, July 13
  • Europe · nine states and Ukraine form a missile-defence coalitionLeaders of Denmark, France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Norway, Spain, Sweden, the UK and Ukraine met in Paris and declared an Integrated Anti-Ballistic Missile Coalition, citing "the growing threat" of ballistic missiles. Zelensky said partners could field a mass-produced, low-cost system within 12 months, built around Ukraine's Freyja programme — pitched as a cheaper Patriot alternative. It is the direct institutional answer to last week's interceptor shortage.
  • UK · Burnham's path clearsBy July 13, 379 Labour MPs — over 80% of the parliamentary party — had nominated Andy Burnham, making a rival nomination mathematically impossible. What was billed as a leadership contest became a coronation.
  • US · Graham's seat filled within 48 hoursSen. Lindsey Graham (R-S.C.) died July 11 at 71 of an aortic dissection. Gov. Henry McMaster appointed Graham's sister Darline on July 13 at Trump's and Majority Leader John Thune's public urging; she was sworn in July 14 as the first woman to represent South Carolina in the Senate. Special primary filing opens July 21–28. The math matters — it leaves Judiciary Republicans one vote to spare on the Blanche confirmation.
  • Japan · Takaichi's first economic blueprintThe PM's first policy blueprint put defense-industry revival and drones in focus, against a ¥370 trillion investment strategy through 2040 that the bond market is already pricing sceptically.
Tuesday, July 14
  • China · exports surge 27% to a record $412bnCustoms data showed exports up 27% y/y — fastest since October 2021, against 18.2% expected — to a record $412.39bn, on AI hardware demand and tariff front-running. Imports jumped 36% to a record $286.76bn; the surplus hit $125.62bn, with the EU surplus widening 27% to a record $32.9bn. Semiconductors, rare earths, autos and ships led; toys, footwear, steel and furniture lagged. Exports are now effectively the only thing carrying the economy — which is why Wednesday's GDP print landed as hard as it did.
  • China · DeepSeek moves toward an IPOReports emerged that DeepSeek has begun IPO preparations, could file as soon as this year, and is in talks at a pre-money valuation of at least RMB 480bn (~$71bn) — weeks after a reported $7bn round. Sourced to unnamed people familiar; DeepSeek has not confirmed. The company whose chip project triggered last week's global rout is now raising against it.
  • Japan · JGB yields hit 30-year highsThe 10-year JGB yield reached 2.865–2.901%, the highest in roughly 30 years and unseen since 1996 — up over 70bp year-to-date. Former BOJ policymaker Seiji Adachi warned the bank "could face pressure to expand bond purchases if the 10-year JGB yield rises above 3%."
Wednesday, July 15
  • China · Q2 GDP misses Beijing's own target — the first since CovidQ2 GDP rose 4.3% y/y, below the 4.5% consensus and beneath the government's 4.5–5.0% full-year band, itself the least ambitious target in decades. H1 GDP was RMB 69.57tn (~$10.25tn), up 4.7%. The composition is the story: industrial output beat at +5.3% and retail returned to growth at +1.0%, but fixed-asset investment contracted 5.7% in H1, with property investment down ~18%. New-home prices fell for a 36th straight month. NBS framed it as "operating within an appropriate range"; independent outlets led on the miss.
  • Türkiye · the 10th anniversary of July 15 becomes a live battlegroundErdoğan marked the coup attempt's decade at the TBMM, praying for "253 martyrs" and vowing to fight FETÖ "until totally eradicated" — warning against complacency despite the network losing "guard, hope and motivation." Özgür Özel used the same day to say the reckoning stopped at FETÖ's "political wing," and asked how those who resisted alongside him in 2016 can now "hope for salvation from the butlan process." The commemoration did present-tense political work.
  • Türkiye · Özel names a date for a new partyÖzel's most explicit statement yet: name and logo work is underway and a formal step comes "end of July or beginning of August," pending CHP legal proceedings. A 30-person technical team is preparing the launch; the working name under most consideration is reportedly simply "Yeni Parti." Turkey's main opposition is now visibly splitting.
  • Türkiye · the arrest wave beginsÇankaya Mayor Hüseyin Can Güner was arrested with 23 others in an Ankara tender probe — his lawyer says the case rests on secret-witness testimony. Singer Haluk Levent and 13 others were arrested over the Ahbap charity, which collected ~$158m in earthquake donations.
  • US · two confirmation hearings go badlyTodd Blanche, acting AG and Trump's former personal lawyer, was grilled over the $10bn IRS settlement and Epstein-file redactions. DNI nominee Jay Clayton repeatedly refused to say Biden won in 2020 — "I am not an election denier. Joe Biden was certified" — which Sen. Ossoff called "disqualifying."
  • US · House Democrats split in half on Israel aidThe Massie amendment to zero out $3.3bn in Israel security aid failed 104–314 — but 103 Democrats voted for it, 98 against. Pelosi and Whip Clark broke with Jeffries.
  • Hong Kong · third bookstore raid in four monthsPolice raided two Mong Kok shops and arrested five over allegedly seditious publications. No titles were specified.
  • Global · Pew finds China now outpolls the USChina rated more favorably than the US in 25 of 36 countries surveyed — the first such reversal in about 20 years. Median confidence in Xi exceeds Trump by 10 points.
  • France · assisted dying becomes lawThe National Assembly gave final approval 291–241, completing legislation Macron announced over three years ago.
Thursday, July 16
  • Mid-East · the war expands toward TehranThe interim ceasefire collapsed and the US expanded strikes northward toward Tehran, firing on a ship said to be breaching the naval blockade. Iran reported 35+ killed and 300+ wounded; targets included Semnan missile and space facilities, Greater Tunb Island, and rail and bridge infrastructure near Bandar Abbas. IRGC spokesman Col. Ebrahim Zolfaghari on the strait: "This is Iran's invincible red line."
  • Markets · TSMC's best quarter ever — and the stock fell anywayTSMC posted Q2 net profit up 77.4% to a record NT$706.6bn, revenue +36% to $40.2bn, raised capex to $60–64bn and guided full-year growth "slightly above 40%" — plus $100bn more for Arizona ($265bn total). Shares fell more than 4%, dragging semiconductors down globally. Shanghai and Shenzhen closed at three-month lows. When record earnings can't lift a stock, the repricing is of expectations, not fundamentals.
  • EU · Brussels forces Google openTwo binding DMA specification decisions require Google to give third-party AI assistants the same Android access Gemini enjoys — including voice activation and in-app actions — and to share anonymised Search ranking, query, click and view data with rivals, beginning by January 2027. Google warned it "risks undermining vital privacy and security guardrails." The most consequential DMA enforcement yet.
  • US · Trump alleges Chinese election theft in primetimeTrump claimed China ran "the largest compromise of election data in history," alleging access to 220 million voter files. The government's own 2021 intelligence assessment concluded with high confidence China did not attempt to influence the outcome. ABC, NBC and CNN declined to air it on their primary channels. Beijing: it "has never and will never interfere."
  • US · DOJ subpoenas Times journalists; the Times moves to quashThree of five expected reporters were served over Air Force One coverage — that the Secret Service urged the switch to the Qatari-gifted jet, which lacked some antimissile capabilities despite a $400m retrofit. Deputy general counsel David McCraw alleged "bad faith to punish The Times for its coverage."
  • Korea · BOK hikes for the first time in 3.5 yearsA unanimous board lifted the base rate 25bp to 2.75%, reversing the easing cycle begun October 2024, on 3.1% CPI and household debt. All upcoming meetings called "live." The hike pressured Samsung and SK Hynix and spilled into Japanese chip names.
  • Japan · a ¥1 trillion national AI betNvidia and Noetra (SoftBank, Sony, NEC, Honda + 44 investors) will build a 140MW AI factory with 27,500 Rubin GPUs — the compute base for METI's FRONTia physical-AI program, funded up to ¥1tn over five years. Japan targets 30%+ of the global AI robotics market by 2040. It landed the same week markets punished the AI trade.
  • Europe · Genoa verdict, 8 years onEx-Atlantia CEO Giovanni Castellucci was sentenced to 12 years over the Morandi bridge collapse that killed 43 in 2018; around 30 defendants were convicted. Milan prosecutors separately raided Bulgari, Chanel, Moncler and others over subcontractors' exploitation of Chinese workers.
  • Europe · Britain nationalises British SteelThe UK took the loss-making firm from Chinese owner Jingye Group to protect Scunthorpe production. Beijing said it "firmly opposes" a move it called forcible, warning it "severely undermined" Chinese confidence in investing in Britain.
  • US · climate and health strainSmoke from 1,000+ Canadian fires pushed air quality to hazardous from the Midwest to the East Coast — roughly 100 million under air-quality alerts and another 100 million under heat advisories. Texas Hill Country flash floods killed at least 2, one year after the 2025 disaster. CDC and FDA linked a cyclospora outbreak (~1,645 confirmed Taco Bell-linked cases) to Taylor Farms lettuce. Measles hit 2,231 cases — a 30-year high, 58 shy of all of 2025.
Friday, July 17 today
  • Markets · the AI rout reaches TokyoThe Nikkei fell 4.03% to 64,140, briefly down over 4,100 points intraday — its worst level in a month, off 6.4% on the week. Tokyo Electron −8.2%, SoftBank −9%, Taiyo Yuden −11.9%. Kioxia fell as much as 16%, now down 52% from its June peak — roughly ¥30tn (~$185bn) erased from a stock that had risen 600% this year and briefly passed Toyota in market cap. A Texas jury also ordered it to pay Viasat $229m.
  • China · Xi's first WAIC keynote, and a new AI blocXi told the World AI Conference in Shanghai that China would "encourage open source, openness, collaboration and sharing," called for governance that stays "secure and controllable," and pledged 5,000 training places for developing countries. A day earlier, 29 countries signed the founding agreement for the World AI Cooperation Organization (WAICO), headquartered in Shanghai — Russia, Belarus, Serbia, Cuba, Brazil, Venezuela, 10 African and 12 Asian states. The US and major EU economies are absent. Set against Brussels' Google order 24 hours earlier, AI governance now has two competing centres.
  • UK · Burnham becomes Labour leader; PM on MondayConfirmed unopposed at a special conference, Burnham succeeds Keir Starmer — who resigned last month after poor May local results — and is expected in office Monday July 20 as the UK's seventh prime minister in a decade, with no general election behind it. He pledged to be "unashamedly Labour"; Badenoch accused Labour of "running scared" of scrutiny. (Euronews dates this July 19/22; NBC, US News and Wikipedia say 17/20 — the majority reading is used.)
  • Japan · male-only imperial succession enactedThe House of Councillors passed the first substantive revision of the Imperial House Law in 79 years, entrenching paternal-line male-only succession and excluding Princess Aiko, 24. It permits adopting male descendants from 11 branch families that renounced status in 1947 — some 36 generations removed. Polls show 60–90% support for female emperors. Scholar Chizuko Ueno called it "ironic" that Japan's first female PM led the effort. The Diet also enacted a flag-desecration law (up to 2 years' imprisonment) over the objections of 148 legal experts.
  • EU · the Green Deal's core instrument bendsThe Commission proposed cutting the ETS annual cap reduction to ~3.7% from 2031 (down from 4.3%) and extending free allowances to end-2037 instead of 2034 — likely delaying the carbon border levy. It follows a July 15 declaration by ten member states representing just over 35% of EU population: enough for a Council blocking minority. The clearest rollback to date.
  • Ukraine · seven killed; the shadow fleet hit at scaleRussian attacks killed 7 and injured 67 in 24 hours, with Odesa struck again in peak tourist season. Ukraine said a large-scale operation damaged 12 Russian shadow-fleet vessels — nine dry cargo ships, a tanker, a gas carrier, a tug — alongside strikes across occupied Crimea. Zelensky meanwhile sacked defence minister Fedorov after six months, naming SBU general Yevhen Khmara acting successor — awkward against a law requiring a civilian. Protests followed in several cities; Russian milbloggers welcomed it.
  • Türkiye · lira at 47.05 ahead of a consequential holdThe dollar traded at 47.05 TL. June CPI was 32.11% y/y (0.99% m/m), core 29.84%. The TCMB decides July 23 — and with no August meeting on the calendar, that statement guides markets to September. Consensus: no change.
  • Asia · Taiwan pressure, on two frontsPNG said Taiwan's representative office would close immediately; Taipei says it stays open and is reassessing ties — a factual standoff, not just a diplomatic one. Taiwan's MAC separately said two police officers were seized and interrogated in Fujian about critical-infrastructure lists. Wang Huning met Kim Jong Un in Pyongyang, pledging Chinese support "will never change."
  • Europe · heat, fire and droughtSpain's Aragón wildfire destroyed 4,500+ hectares and reached the urban area of Asín; Italy was set to put 19 cities on red alert, with June excess mortality among over-65s at 3%. The Netherlands declared an official water shortage. A forest fire in İzmir's Torbalı forced evacuation of an industrial zone.
