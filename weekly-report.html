Running Threads

Iran war & the Middle East

Friday update (June 5): The US–Iran ceasefire from the 2026 war (US/Israel strikes began Feb. 28; an April 7–8 ceasefire never hardened into a settlement) is fraying at the edges. Trump told reporters a deal could come "this weekend," but Iran's foreign minister countered there had been "no significant process." On June 4 a new US-brokered Israel–Lebanon ceasefire was announced — and immediately undercut: Hezbollah, not a party to it, rejected the pact, and an Israeli soldier was killed by an anti-tank missile in southern Lebanon on day one. In Washington, the House passed a war-powers resolution constraining Trump's authority to keep prosecuting the conflict (four Republicans crossing) — CNN called it one of the biggest legislative rebukes of his presidency, landing as his approval hits historic lows.

Ukraine — aid, sanctions, and a European diplomatic push

Thursday–Friday (June 4–5): The House passed ~$1B+ in Ukraine security/reconstruction aid plus $8B in loans and steep new Russia sanctions, 226–195, with 18 Republicans defying Speaker Johnson and the White House; the bill now faces a 60-vote Senate hurdle and a veto threat. In parallel, Germany, France and the UK are coordinating with Kyiv on a plan to draw Putin to the table (Bloomberg), sensing momentum has shifted to Zelenskyy amid a battlefield stalemate. On the ground: Russian drones hit Odesa infrastructure, Zaporizhzhia, Dnipro (Nova Post terminal again) and Kharkiv overnight into June 4; Ukraine struck Russian UAV crews at the Donetsk Airport logistics hub and a Svetlyak-class patrol ship in occupied Crimea. Ukrainian drones struck St. Petersburg on June 3, hours before SPIEF opened.

Türkiye — inflation and the CHP leadership war

Friday update (June 5): TÜİK reported May CPI at +1.71% m/m / +32.61% y/y — a seven-month high — with PPI at +28.93% y/y; the June rent-increase ceiling is set at 32.24%. The CHP's internal crisis continues to dominate: after the May 21 court ruling annulled the 38th congress and reinstated Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu as chair (sidelining Özgür Özel), the reinstated leadership's disciplinary board halted all of Özel's expulsions. A "group meeting" power struggle followed — Özel convened the CHP parliamentary group despite Kılıçdaroğlu's instruction otherwise. 111 CHP MPs are demanding an extraordinary congress as soon as possible (July 12 floated); watch the dates June 9 and 11. İmamoğlu remains jailed.

Markets & the AI cap-table

Week through Thursday (June 4): A risk-on week with a notable internal rotation. The S&P closed above 7,600 for the first time on June 2 on a chip-led rally; by June 3 the Dow surged ~900 points to a record, and on June 4 it set a fresh record at 51,561.93 (+1.73%) as money rotated out of chips into value (UnitedHealth +5%, JPMorgan +3%) — leaving the Nasdaq down 0.09% at 26,830.96 and the S&P +0.41% at 7,584.31. Brent eased to about $97/bbl on tentative Iran-deal hopes. On the AI cap table: Anthropic confidentially filed for an IPO on June 1 and released Claude Opus 4.8 (with a cheaper fast mode and expanded security tooling); OpenAI is reportedly weighing its own offering; and Microsoft launched its own MAI models (including MAI-Code-1-Flash) to reduce reliance on OpenAI.

EU enlargement & tech sovereignty

Tuesday → Friday: The Commission unveiled a European technological sovereignty package (June 3) targeting semiconductors, AI, cloud and open source. On June 5 the EU–Western Balkans summit convened in Tivat, Montenegro — the highest-level enlargement gathering in years, with von der Leyen, Costa, Macron, Merz and Kallas. Expected: an enlargement-acceleration package (added pre-accession funding, streamlined chapter assessments, EU–Balkans free-roaming talks), with Montenegro and Albania the frontrunners and Costa pushing to drop the unanimity requirement at each accession stage.

Health watch — New World screwworm

June 3–4: USDA APHIS confirmed the first US detection of New World screwworm in a 3-week-old calf in Zavala County, Texas (larvae in the umbilical area). A 12-mile perimeter is in place; sterile-fly releases and movement controls are planned. No further detections to date; the food supply is unaffected. Elsewhere, June brought a run of medical headlines — a KRAS inhibitor (daraxonrasib) nearly doubling pancreatic-cancer survival, GLP-1 drugs linked to reduced addiction in a 600,000-veteran study, and a newly mapped amygdala anxiety circuit.
