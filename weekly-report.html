Weekly Report

Week of August 3 – 9, 2026

A running log of the week's major stories — updated each day. Revision: Friday, August 7. Where last week left off (July 27 – August 2): the Iran war ran pause → collapse → a two-hour barrage with Saudi participation → a second pause announced late on Saturday 1 August while Kuwait was shooting down drones. Gen. Alexus Grynkewich had warned the Pentagon in writing that without another destroyer he must choose between defending the US homeland and defending Israel. Zelensky said Ukraine had stopped one ballistic missile out of 27 "simply because there are no interceptors." The Ceuta border gave way — 67 dead, ~69,500 returned to Morocco in 30 hours — and 22 EU leaders demanded an emergency interior ministers' meeting, with France, Portugal and Luxembourg declining to sign. Kumamoto's earthquake toll reached 36; Türkiye's policy rate was frozen at 37% until 10 September; China's Politburo produced no stimulus package against a 49.2 PMI. What this week has established so far: the binding constraint on both wars is the same interceptor and long-range-missile inventory, and it is now measurable — "virtually all" ATACMS gone, ~80% of THAAD, ~half of Patriot. Everything else on this page follows from that number. Friday added the consequence: Saudi Arabia, Türkiye and Pakistan signed a mutual-defence treaty in Mecca, and US payrolls contracted by 23,000. A Gulf security architecture that does not route through Washington and a Federal Reserve that voted three times to hike six weeks before the labour market shrank are, in different registers, the same story — the assumed supplier of security and of demand is turning out to have limits.

Threads to Watch — live through the weekend

  • The Mecca pact is the week's structural eventThree states with genuinely complementary assets — NATO's second-largest army, the only nuclear-armed Muslim state, the Arab world's only G20 economy — signed an attack-on-one clause in Islam's holiest city, after a year of negotiation, one day after Riyadh warned of imminent strikes on its ports and airports. Officially aimed at no one. Read regionally as aimed at exactly one country. Three things decide whether it is architecture or symbolism: whether the full text is published; whether "armed attack" is defined to cover drone and missile strikes on civilian and energy infrastructure, which is the actual threat Saudi Arabia faces and therefore the clause that binds or doesn't; and whether a fourth state accedes. Then the reactions that had not landed by Friday evening — Tehran, Jerusalem, New Delhi, Washington. And note what it implies about the ammunition ledger below: a Gulf security arrangement that does not route through Washington is a rational response to a supplier that is rationing.
  • The Fed's problem is now arithmetic, not rhetoricOn 29 July the FOMC held 9–3 with all three dissents wanting to hike. On 7 August payrolls printed −23,000 against +80k expected, with June cut from +57k to +20k — two consecutive downward revisions and an outright contraction. The unemployment rate fell to 4.1% anyway, which means the household and establishment surveys are telling different stories and participation is doing the work. Yields fell across the curve; September hike odds were already down to 44.5% before the print. Either the inflation impulse the hawks were reacting to is now colliding with a labour market that has stopped adding jobs, or the payroll survey is distorted — and both cases will be argued loudly before September. Watch the next CPI print and whether any of the three dissenters speaks publicly.
  • The inventory is the story, and it is now a number"Virtually all" ATACMS and PrSM expended; ~80% of THAAD; ~half of Patriot; restocking into 2027 at quadrupled output; CENTCOM pulling from other theatres. On the same 24 hours, Kyiv took 24 ballistic missiles and stopped none. Two wars, one interceptor pool, and commanders openly rationing. Every diplomatic move this week — the Omani corridor, the sixth night without a strike, the 60-day no-toll framing — is more parsimoniously explained by that ledger than by any change of heart. Watch whether Washington publishes the joint statement, and watch Hormuz transit counts rather than communiqués.
  • A deal Tehran cannot call a dealIran insists it is negotiating shipping coordinates with Oman, not terms with America, while hardliners assemble impeachment paperwork against Araghchi and float the same against Pezeshkian. That is the constraint on durability: an arrangement whose Iranian domestic description must remain "a technical route agreement with a neighbour" has very little tolerance for a public American victory lap. Watch the wording of the joint statement, if it appears. Friday sharpened this considerably. The plan actually before the Majlis is not a routing arrangement at all: a ban on US-, Israel- and other "hostile"-linked vessels, tolls of up to 7% of cargo value, 20% fines for violations, and compensation payable before passage by designated states. Washington has rejected it. So there are two documents — technical corridors Oman can broker, and a toll schedule that is a sovereignty claim over an international strait — and each capital is describing whichever one suits it while both say "close." Brent at $82 is priced off the first document. If the Majlis passes the second, that price is wrong.
