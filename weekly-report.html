A running log of the week's major stories — updated each day. Latest revision: Thursday, June 25. Where last week left off: the June US–Iran war had moved from open conflict to a paper truce — the "Islamabad memorandum" ended hostilities, lifted the naval blockade and reopened the Strait of Hormuz, with ~$24bn of frozen assets pledged over a 60-day nuclear-talks window. This week opens with that all coming to a head.

Daily Log

Monday, June 22
  • UK · Starmer resignsKeir Starmer announced his resignation as prime minister and Labour leader after a parliamentary-party revolt — Britain's seventh leader in ten years — saying he had informed King Charles III and would stay on as caretaker. The trigger was Andy Burnham's June 18 Makerfield by-election win, which returned the ex-Manchester mayor to the Commons; Burnham declared his candidacy within hours and rival Wes Streeting endorsed him. Nominations open July 9, close July 16, leader by Sept 1 if contested; a Manchester mayoral by-election is set for July 30.
  • Iran · roadmapThe first high-level Bürgenstock round ended with a "roadmap" to a final deal within 60 days (mediators Pakistan/Qatar citing "encouraging progress"), plus working groups on sanctions, the nuclear file and oversight, a Hormuz "communication line" and an Israel–Lebanon deconfliction cell. VP Vance called it "a very good day" and prepared to hand off to Witkoff and Kushner — but the sides immediately split on substance: Vance said Iran agreed to readmit IAEA inspectors, which Tehran disputed.
  • Oil · sanctionsThe de-escalation drained the premium: Brent fell toward ~$78 (a 3-month low), WTI toward ~$74, after the US temporarily eased Iranian oil sanctions through Aug 21. CENTCOM said 55 ships transited Hormuz June 20 (17M+ barrels) and reiterated "Iran does not control the Strait."
  • Trade · ChinaBeijing retaliated for a Pentagon blacklist (Alibaba, Baidu) by sanctioning 10 US defence firms — Red Cat, Teal Drones, Oshkosh Defense, L3Harris Maritime, Ball Aerospace, MP Materials, USA Rare Earth — barring Chinese dual-use exports to them. Trump separately signed quantum and cryptography executive orders and threatened to "hit Iran very hard again."
  • China · PBOCThe central bank held the LPR for a 13th straight month (1-yr 3.0%, 5-yr 3.5%) against a soft backdrop (May retail sales −0.6% y/y). The Shanghai Composite rose ~1.8% to ~4,164, its highest since May.
  • Japan · marketsThe Nikkei 225 closed at a record 72,353.96 (+1.55%) — first-ever above 72,000, a seventh straight record — on a ¥10.5tn "physical AI" plan and a weak yen, while USD/JPY sat near ¥161.5, a whisker from its 1986 low. FM Katayama held virtual talks with US Treasury's Bessent; no new intervention confirmed.
  • US · marketsWall Street closed mixed: S&P 500 −0.37% to 7,472.79, Nasdaq ~−1.3% on Big Tech weakness (Alphabet −5–7%), Dow +0.2% — and the Russell 2000 closed at 3,000 for the first time ever on a rotation into small caps. Getty Images jumped ~90% on an OpenAI licensing deal; SpaceX launched a ≥$20bn debut bond sale.
  • ClimateA solstice heatwave turned deadly across western Europe: France confirmed several heat deaths (two children found dead in a car in Carpentras) with 49 of 96 departments on top alert; the UK logged its hottest June day on record (~38°C) and Iberia neared 44°C.
  • Ukraine warRussian strikes killed at least 10 across Ukraine over 24 hours (89 drones launched, 79 downed); in Sumy a drone killed a 13-year-old boy, his father and grandmother.
  • SportMessi scored twice in Argentina's 2–0 win over Austria for his 18th career World Cup goal — an all-time record; France beat Iraq 3–0 (Mbappé brace), Norway edged Senegal 3–2 (Haaland brace). At Shinnecock, Wyndham Clark won the US Open, surviving a near-collapse to beat Sam Burns by one.

Technical talks continued at Bürgenstock.
