Running Threads

Trump in Beijing — Day 2 produces joint statement, Hormuz pledge, Washington return visit

After a 2-hour 15-minute bilateral at the Great Hall of the People on May 14, Trump and Xi released a joint statement committing both governments to a "constructive China-U.S. relationship of strategic stability" guiding the next three years. Xi explicitly warned that mishandling Taiwan could lead to "clashes and even conflicts," invoking the Thucydides Trap. Both sides pledged the Strait of Hormuz must remain open and non-militarised; Xi expressed interest in increasing U.S. oil imports to reduce Chinese dependence on the strait. Trump invited Xi for a return state visit to Washington on September 24, 2026. The U.S. tech-CEO delegation — Musk, Cook, Huang — accompanied Rubio. Commerce greenlit Nvidia H200 sales to ~10 Chinese firms (Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance, JD.com) on the eve of the meeting, though no deliveries yet. Rare-earth October framework remains in force pending an extension announcement; the rumoured $14B Taiwan arms package is still the most dangerous fault line. Markets read Day 2 as more "stabilising" than "breakthrough."

Iran war & Hormuz — first crack opens

The Chinese VLCC Yuan Hua Hu sailed out of the strait past Iran's Larak Island into the Gulf of Oman on May 13 — the rare exit timed to Trump-Xi summit prep, consistent with Iran's March 26 carve-out for China/Russia/India/Iraq/Pakistan-flagged vessels. But it's one tanker, not a reopening. Strait operating well below normal capacity May 11-13; Gen. Caine confirmed 22,500 mariners on >1,550 vessels still stranded. ADNOC's Sultan Al Jaber: Iran imposing >$1M/ship tolls, ~230 tankers waiting inside the Gulf. Trump's May 6 pause of "Project Freedom" still holds. U.S. cost-of-war tracker hit $29B (Pentagon comptroller). EIA still warning crude could rise another $20/bbl if the chokepoint persists. Pump prices ~$1.50/gal above pre-war baseline.

Russia–Ukraine — record overnight assault on Kyiv

The May 9–11 US-brokered ceasefire expired late on the 11th. Overnight May 11–12, Russia fired 216 drones; overnight May 13–14 the assault escalated to ~56 missiles + 675 Shahed-type drones — the single largest aerial assault of the war — 5 killed and 44+ injured in Kyiv, Kharkiv also hit. The 30-hour, ~1,600-munition blitz was timed to Trump's Beijing arrival. Ukraine struck back at Russian gas-industry sites in Orenburg, 1,500 km inside Russia, earlier in the week. FM Sybiha is still pushing a European-brokered mutual airport-strike pause. Trump administration silent on the strikes through Thursday morning. Cumulative Russian troop losses since Feb 24 2022: 1,343,050.

UK political crisis — Streeting walks

By May 12, 88 Labour MPs and four junior ministers had called on PM Starmer to step down after the May local-election losses (~1,500 council seats, mostly to Reform UK). Streeting met Starmer privately May 12, then publicly backed off a challenge. On May 14, Streeting formally resigned as Health Secretary in a letter asserting Starmer "will not lead the Labour Party into the next general election" — the first cabinet-rank exit and the closest thing to a leadership-bid setup short of declaring. The resignation count is now five (four junior + Streeting). Eurasia Group keeps the 80% ouster probability for 2026. Gilt yields are climbing. UK Q1 GDP printed +0.6% q/q this morning (best in over a year, beat 0.4% forecast), briefly steadying the economic argument. Starmer pledged a "complete break," nationalising British Steel and pivoting closer to Brussels.

Neurotech — Paradromics into humans

On May 14, Paradromics completed its first in-human implant of the Connexus BCI at the University of Michigan — a 420-electrode device placed in the temporal lobe of an epilepsy patient already in surgery, ~20 min implant+remove time, recording at single-neuron resolution. Surgeon: Dr. Oren Sagher. Research lead: Dr. Matthew Willsey. Connexus reports >200 bps (≈20× Neuralink's initial demo). Neuralink itself disclosed in April: 45 enrolled across three continents; 85% of spinal-injury participants hitting pick-and-place within 150% of non-injured baselines; next-gen surgical robot capable of reaching virtually any brain region. Cambridge hafnium-oxide neuromorphic chip claim from May 13: 70% AI-energy cut.

Quantum — Q-CTRL/IBM remains the "useful advantage" anchor; Kyoto's W-state breakthrough

Q-CTRL × IBM: 3,000× speedup on 120-qubit Fermi-Hubbard materials simulation — still the standing "useful quantum advantage" benchmark. JUPITER (Germany) classically simulates a 50-qubit machine. Google Willow Quantum Echoes 13,000× over classical, in *Nature*. New May 13: Kyoto University + Hiroshima University built a tabletop photonic circuit that performs one-shot identification of 3-photon W states by exploiting cyclic shift symmetry through a photonic quantum-Fourier-transform circuit. The method generalises to any number of photons — unblocks a long-standing bottleneck for scalable quantum networks. Harvard separately: field advancing faster than expected. IBM's May 4 "Decade of Quantum on the Cloud" anchors the broader trajectory.
