Weekly Report

Week of July 27 – August 2, 2026

A running log of the week's major stories — updated each day. Final revision: Sunday, August 2 — this week is now closed. Where last week left off (July 20 – 26): the Iran war ran into a fourth week before Washington abruptly paused its strikes, and Brent fell from above $100 toward $85 on Pakistani mediation reports. Britain's Andy Burnham took office as its seventh prime minister in a decade and received Zelensky aboard a carrier within a week. Türkiye's opposition split when Özgür Özel and 91 deputies resigned from the CHP on 24 July to found Yeni Parti. The TCMB held at 37% with no August meeting scheduled, and China's Q2 GDP of 4.3% missed Beijing's own target. How this week ended: the pause collapsed into a two-hour barrage and then reassembled itself into a second pause on Saturday night; the AI trade crashed and staged the largest single-day value gain in market history; an earthquake killed 36 people in Kyushu; a Russian cruise missile detonated inside Poland without triggering Article 4; and the Morocco–Spain border at Ceuta gave way, producing 67 deaths and the first 22-nation EU migration letter of this cycle.

Daily Log

Monday, July 27
  • Iran · the pause reaches a third nightThe US held off lethal strikes for a third straight night while Reuters reported Pakistan preparing to broker fresh US–Iran talks. Brent fell 11% in a single session to $85.48. Tehran's public line stayed colder — Iran said it had no negotiations with the United States at present. Iranian and Omani deputy foreign ministers met in Tehran on "management of shipping traffic" through Hormuz; the IRGC navy stopped six vessels attempting unapproved transits. Fewer than 10 ships passed the strait, against roughly 100 in a normal week.
  • China · CXMT's debut prices a decade of self-sufficiency policyChangXin Memory Technologies raised ¥57.92bn (~$8.6bn) at ¥8.66 a share — Asia's largest IPO of 2026 and the biggest semiconductor listing ever on the STAR Market — then rose 466–472% on debut to roughly a $489bn valuation. A parallel report said China had begun mass-producing domestic immersion DUV lithography tools, with first deliveries this year to SMIC, Hua Hong and CXMT.
  • Markets · cheaper oil lifted everything except technologyDow +0.5% to 51,947.25, S&P 500 +0.05% to 7,411.98, Nasdaq lower. Real estate and materials led; technology was the worst sector — the first clean reading of the rotation that would define the week.
  • UK · Zelensky aboard HMS Queen ElizabethA week into office, Burnham received Volodymyr Zelensky at Portsmouth — the first foreign-leader visit of his premiership. Joint drone production and Ukraine's ballistic-missile interceptor gap were the agenda; the venue was the message.
  • Europe · a fourth heatwave and 250,000 evacuatedFrance recorded 3.4× its annual average number of fires; Spain 1.7×. The Gironde blaze pushed toward Bordeaux's suburbs, repeatedly generating its own lightning. Iceland opened early voting for its 29 August referendum on restarting EU accession talks.
Tuesday, July 28
  • Japan · a magnitude-7.1 earthquake hits KumamotoAt 16:27 JST a quake struck Kumamoto Prefecture on Kyushu — MJMA 7.1 (USGS later Mww 6.8), 10km deep, registering shindo 7, the top of the Japanese intensity scale. About 3,000 shoppers had been evacuated from the Aeon Mall in Kashima before a gas explosion tore through another part of the building; part of a smokestack collapsed at the Nippon Paper Industries plant in Yatsushiro. The initial toll was 13. It would rise every day this week.
  • Asia · the memory crashSeoul's Kospi fell 11% to 6,012.68 with trading halted mid-session — Samsung −13.1%, SK Hynix −13.5%. Tokyo's Nikkei lost 4.3% to 62,127.57; Taiwan's Taiex 4.7%. Shanghai fell only 1.4%, and the asymmetry was the signal: investors were pricing who loses from Chinese memory capacity.
  • Iran · the IRGC fires on US forces in JordanIran struck US assets in Jordan — Jordanian air defences intercepted five missiles in what CENTCOM called an "attempted surprise attack" — saying it was answering "aggressive actions" by the US military. Trump promised to hit Iran "very hard." The four-night pause was over.
  • Türkiye · Baghdad in Ankara, with Iran the unspoken agendaIraq's prime minister made an official visit at Erdoğan's invitation, with a Turkey–Iraq business round table alongside. The formal agenda was trade and the Development Road corridor; the real one was a war on both countries' borders and energy routing while Hormuz stayed effectively shut.
Wednesday, July 29
  • US · a 9–3 Fed hold with three dissentsThe FOMC left the band at 3.50–3.75%. Beth Hammack, Neel Kashkari and Lorie Logan all dissented with inflation above target for more than five years. Chair Kevin Warsh called it a "good family fight"; markets were rattled rather than reassured.
  • Earnings · the AI-capex verdict splitsMicrosoft reported Azure +43% and rose 8% after hours — while disclosing that about two-thirds of its capex goes to "short-lived assets, primarily CPUs and GPUs." Meta fell 8% after raising the floor of its 2026 capex range to $130–145bn and missing EPS on one-time legal and severance items. SK Hynix posted record quarterly revenue at a 76% operating margin.
  • Iran · "our turn" — a two-hour heavy waveLate Wednesday CENTCOM struck dozens of IRGC targets inside Iran: command centres, missile and drone facilities, coastal surveillance and defence sites, maritime capabilities. Saudi Arabia joined US strikes on Iran-aligned groups — the first time a Gulf state has been an active participant rather than a hedging bystander.
  • Indo-Pacific · the coastguard moves east of TaiwanReporting detailed repeated PRC coastguard patrols and "special maritime law-enforcement operations" in waters east of Taiwan through June and July — the island's Pacific side, and the first time in such quick succession. Analysts read it as rehearsal for containment rather than invasion.
Thursday, July 30
  • Poland · a Russian cruise missile detonates on NATO soilDuring Russia's overnight barrage a missile crossed into Polish airspace and exploded near Tarnawa-Kolonia outside Lublin, leaving a 10-metre crater; Polish authorities said all indications point to a Kh-101. Two NATO fighters, a NATO tanker, a Polish AEW aircraft and a helicopter were scrambled — NATO says it was seconds from shooting the missile down before assessing it would fall on its own. Tusk held emergency talks. No Article 4 invocation. In Ukraine the same barrage killed 8–10 civilians and injured more than 50.
  • US · growth 1.5%, GDP price index 6.3%Q2 GDP slowed from 2.1% and missed forecasts, while the quarterly deflator printed 6.3% against 3.6% expected. Monthly data pulled the other way: PCE −0.1% in June, annual 3.7% from 4.1%, core 3.3%, consumption +3.2%, jobless claims 197,000. The stagflation word got used out loud.
  • Markets · the largest single-day gain by any stock, everMicrosoft rose 16%, adding roughly $450bn. Dow +1.2% to 52,208.06, S&P +1.7% to 7,437.63, Nasdaq +2.8% to 25,122.18 — ending a six-session losing streak. After the bell Amazon posted its first $200bn quarter ($200.6bn, +20%) with AWS at $42.2bn, +37%, its fastest in 18 quarters. Apple beat on both lines and fell more than 6% on weak China sales and guided margin compression.
  • UK · a 6–3 Bank of England hold, with three voting to hikeRates stayed at 3.75% for the fifth time this year; Greene, Pill and Mann wanted +25bp. Bailey: "Please do not leave this room thinking that the Bank of England is edging towards a hike."
  • China · a Politburo that promised acceleration, not stimulusLeaders acknowledged "difficulties and challenges," committed to faster fiscal spending and bond issuance, "incremental policies," and the Six Networks build-out above ¥21.9tn — but named no RRR or rate cut. The revived phrase on "timely adjusting monetary policy tools" firmed expectations of an easing window near end-Q3. Coal fell below 50% of electricity output for the first time (49.7% in H1).
  • Japan · a food tax cut, and a new intelligence bureauTakaichi decided to cut the consumption tax on food and soft drinks to 1% from 8% for two years from April 2027 — a first — as her approval fell from above 70% to the low-40s/high-50s range. Japan also moved to stand up a National Intelligence Bureau. The Kumamoto toll passed 25, then 30.
  • Middle East · rare progress in CairoMediators and Hamas leaders reported progress toward decommissioning all heavy and light weapons in Gaza; Trump announced an agreement had been reached for complete disarmament. Hamas's Razi Hamad said weapons storage remains contingent on gradual Israeli withdrawal — not the same description of the same deal. Israeli strikes killed four to six Palestinians, including two children.
  • Türkiye · a detention order for a pro-government journalistİstanbul prosecutors ordered the detention of commentator Cem Küçük for "publicly disseminating misleading information" and "extortion" — reportedly over paid social-media posts and statements made during the İBB investigation.
Friday, July 31
  • Korea · a 16.5% dayThe Kospi surged to 6,515.40, with Samsung +24.8% and SK Hynix +27.8% — three days after falling 17%. Nikkei +5.5% to 65,282.21, SoftBank +15%, Tokyo Electron +11%. The index remains far below the 9,000-plus it reached in June: a three-day 17% fall and a one-day 16.5% rise are not a round trip, they are evidence that memory has no stable price.
  • Japan · the BOJ holds at 1%, and Tokyo appears to have intervenedHajime Takata dissented for a hike. The dollar bought about ¥163.57 after a fresh 40-year low of ¥163.99; reporting indicates the government intervened in the currency market before the announcement.
  • China · factory activity contractsJuly manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 from 50.3, missing 50.0 and ending four months of expansion. New orders dropped to 48.5, the lowest since 2023; production to 49.9. July export growth slowed to 11.2% y/y from 17.3%. The export rush that carried Q2 is unwinding — one day after the Politburo declined to announce a package.
  • Türkiye · the Terror-Free Türkiye commission is seatedBy the 17:00 deadline: AK Parti 21, CHP 10, MHP 4, DEM Parti 4, Yeni Yol 3, and one seat each for DSP, HÜDA PAR and TİP — 48 members. The published allocation does not list Özel's Yeni Parti at all, which does not match the reported post-split parliament; the discrepancy is unresolved. Cem Küçük was detained by gendarmerie. June trade data showed a $10.4bn monthly deficit, up 26.3%, with the twelve-month deficit at $95.8bn. Q2 tourism figures were due from Minister Ersoy at the AKM.
  • Japan · the Kumamoto toll reaches 34More than 4,600 Self-Defense Forces personnel and 480 police from eleven prefectures are deployed; hope of further live rescues is fading. Four bodies have been recovered from the Aeon Mall rubble; five are confirmed dead and four unaccounted for at the Yatsushiro paper plant.
  • Europe · a growth beat, and an inflation flash pendingEuro-area Q2 GDP rose 0.4% q/q (EU +0.5%), improving on Q1. June inflation was 2.8%; Eurostat's July flash was scheduled for today, with Ireland's national flash already up at 3.1% — the first hint of the energy shock feeding back through.
  • Health · the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on recordThe DRC's Ituri epidemic reached roughly 3,200 cases and 1,405 deaths as of 26 July. It is Bundibugyo ebolavirus; licensed vaccines and monoclonals were certified against Zaire. France confirmed an imported case on 24 June; a US citizen was evacuated to Germany in May.
Saturday, August 1
  • Europe · the Ceuta border gives way, and 22 leaders write a letterThe death toll from Thursday night's mass crossing into Spain's North African enclave reached 67 — drownings, and a stampede across a breakwater barrier. Roughly 69,500 people were returned to Morocco within 30 hours; estimates of entries run near 60,000. Meloni and Frederiksen led 22 EU heads of state and government in demanding an emergency videoconference of interior ministers, set by the Irish presidency for Tuesday: "We cannot allow uncontrolled mass crossings, the instrumentalisation of migration or other hybrid threats to create the perception that illegal entry into the European Union is possible." France, Portugal and Luxembourg did not sign. Background: Spain's Supreme Court ruled on 29 June that summary returns cannot be applied to migrants intercepted by sea.
  • Ukraine · 27 ballistic missiles, one interceptionRussia fired 35 missiles — 27 ballistic — and 185 drones overnight. Nine killed in Kyiv, 27–30 injured including four children; seven dead in Darnytskyi, two in Solomianskyi. Zelensky said only one ballistic missile was stopped "simply because there are no interceptors for the Patriot system." Second major attack in 48 hours. Ukrainian forces separately sank a Rosatom-owned container ship in the Black Sea; its 17 crew abandoned it.
  • Iran · Kuwait shoots down drones; the US general says he is out of shipsKuwait's army intercepted Iranian drones from dawn, aimed at "vital" installations including a northern government facility. Two more tankers were hit near Oman and Khasab. Then the disclosure that reframes the whole war: Gen. Alexus Grynkewich, head of US European Command, warned the Pentagon in writing that without another destroyer he must choose the US homeland over Israel. Five destroyers deploy from Rota; on Friday two were at sea and three in port.
  • US · a mass shooting in Twin Falls, and Michigan's water systemsA gunman opened fire at an In-N-Out in Twin Falls, Idaho around 3pm; the shooter died at the scene, apparently self-inflicted. Counts ranged from three dead plus the shooter to four including him, with two to seven injured. Separately Michigan reported cyberattacks on nine water systems, joining Minnesota's 30-plus; the FBI is investigating both, and an FBI/CISA advisory last week named Iranian actors targeting water and wastewater operational controls in at least seven states.
  • Russia · a bomb in a gift boxA woman carrying an IED concealed in a gift box — ball bearings, roughly a kilogram of TNT equivalent — detonated it on the veranda of the Balzi Rossi restaurant on Moscow's Kudrinskaya Square as a guard moved to search her. Three dead including the bomber; 15–21 injured.
  • Sport · UEFA tells Infantino to goAfter the collapse of the FIFA Forward Enterprise plan to sell World Cup commercial rights to private investors, UEFA said FIFA's leadership "has not only lost UEFA's confidence but also that of many other members" and told Gianni Infantino to resign or face a no-confidence vote. "Football is not for sale." Mechanically: 43 of FIFA's 211 associations must request an extraordinary Congress; UEFA has 55 members and has not yet said it will.
  • Disasters · Broad Peak and NazcaNirmal Purja, 43 — fastest ascent of all fourteen 8,000ers, 2019 — and nine expedition members were confirmed dead in an avalanche on Broad Peak in Gilgit-Baltistan, missing since Thursday. Among them Pur Bahadur Gurung, Nadhira Al Harthy (first Omani woman to summit Everest) and Mallory Geis. In Peru, an Aerodiana sightseeing flight over the Nazca Lines crashed at Pueblo Viejo, killing all 13 aboard: seven Italians, two Spaniards, two Germans and both pilots.
  • Türkiye · Erdoğan in Istanbul, and a pipeline rolled overAt the AK Party provincial advisory council at Haliç, Erdoğan put membership at 11,710,000 and said the Terror-Free Türkiye process had come this far "without any road accident." Türkiye and Iraq signed a one-year operational agreement on the Iraq–Turkey Crude Oil Pipeline at 750,000 bpd — a short rollover, not a long-term contract, with Hormuz unreliable. New advertising rules took effect: no targeted ads built on children's data, no illegal gambling ads, and no advertising by tarot readers, fortune tellers or astrologers.
  • Energy · the war shows up in earningsExxonMobil's Q2 profit rose 105.1% to $14.5bn; Chevron also up. The geopolitical premium in crude is now a line item.
Sunday, August 2 today
  • Iran · a second pause, announced mid-attackLate Saturday Trump cancelled a pending strike package on Iran, citing the "perimeters of a deal" and saying Iran "and other Middle Eastern countries" asked him to hold off. He described terms as an "immediate, complete, and total" reopening of Hormuz plus an end to Iran's nuclear programme, contingent on closing fast. The same day: Kuwait intercepting drones, two tankers hit, the State Department urging Americans to leave the region, and Trump threatening further strikes if talks stall. This is the second pause in nine days. The first ended when the IRGC hit US forces in Jordan.
  • Japan · Kumamoto toll reaches 36, and the heat arrivesUp from 34 on Friday. Seven deaths from the Aeon Mall explosion in Kashima, eight at the Nippon Paper mill in Yatsushiro. About 34,900 homes still without power, 15,000 without water, more than 8,800 people in over 400 shelters — with Kumamoto city at 36°C and health officials warning about shelter illness.
  • China · the Politburo confirmed: no packageWeekend readouts confirmed Thursday's meeting produced no major announcement — Beijing is implementing existing tools, pivoting to stability and supply-side growth rather than demand stimulus. That lands against Friday's 49.2 July manufacturing PMI, new orders at 48.5 (lowest since 2023), and July export growth decelerating to 11.2% from 17.3%.
  • Europe · the heat peaks Monday25 of the 27 Italian cities monitored by the Health Ministry go to the highest heat alert on Monday. The heat dome over Iberia and France pushes widely past 40°C, locally toward 45°C, extending to Germany, the Baltic and Central Europe. Fourth heatwave of the summer; France at ~3.4× its annual average fire count.
  • Health · Ebola at 3,605 cases and 1,587 deathsWHO figures as of 30 July, against 1,460 cases and 452 deaths on 1 July. Still Bundibugyo, still no licensed vaccine or treatment for that species. Remdesivir and MBP134 trials began 12 July; Oxford is cleared for Phase 1. CFR near 44%. The curve roughly doubled in a month.
  • Sport · Glasgow closesThe Commonwealth Games close tonight at 9pm BST at the Hydro — Simple Minds headlining, handover to Ahmedabad for 2030. Australia lead with roughly 104 medals and 48 golds ahead of Canada and England; India topped the boxing table with seven golds. Saturday's netball finals: New Zealand 61–54 England, Australia 46–45 Jamaica.
  • Markets · a quiet Sunday before a loud weekStocks closed the week higher after Thursday's record session. Ahead: ISM manufacturing and Palantir Monday; CAT, MRK, MCD, PFE, AMD, AMGN, JOLTS and the trade balance Tuesday; and the July jobs report Friday — consensus +87,500 with unemployment expected to tick to 4.3%, while futures price a September Fed hike.

Threads to Watch — carried into next week

  • Iran — the constraint turned out to be materiel, not politicsThe week ran pause → collapse → two-hour barrage (with Saudi Arabia joining the strikes) → second pause, announced Saturday night while Kuwait was still shooting down drones. What changed the analysis was not the diplomacy but Gen. Grynkewich's written warning that without another destroyer he must choose the homeland over Israel — five hulls at Rota, two at sea. Read it alongside Zelensky's statement that Ukraine has no Patriot interceptors left: one interceptor stock, two theatres, and the arithmetic no longer closes. Watch Hormuz transit counts (14 on Wednesday against single digits the week before, ~100 in peacetime) as the honest indicator, not communiqués.
  • Ceuta — a 22-nation bloc formalised itself inside the EU67 dead, ~69,500 returned in 30 hours, and a letter led by Meloni and Frederiksen that France, Portugal and Luxembourg declined to sign. The interior ministers meet Tuesday. The migration substance matters, but the durable outcome may be procedural: a standing majority that convenes itself and drafts on its own initiative. Watch who attends Tuesday on what terms, and whether the Spanish court ruling of 29 June gets legislated around.
  • AI capex — the market started discriminatingMicrosoft and Amazon spend enormously and showed accelerating cloud revenue; both were rewarded, Microsoft with the largest single-day value gain in market history. Meta spends enormously and showed no new revenue proof; it was punished. Three weeks ago all capex was punished indiscriminately. Watch whether the rule survives the next earnings cycle — Nvidia's print, HBM pricing, and whether Meta's floor moves again. See the deep dive.
  • The eastern flank's moving thresholdRomanian drone shoot-downs on three consecutive days, then a Kh-101 cratering Polish soil near Lublin — and no Article 4. Then Saturday: 27 ballistic missiles at Kyiv and one interception. Each incident stays below the line individually; the line itself is quietly moving, and Ukraine's ability to hold anything below it is now stock-limited rather than doctrine-limited. Watch what the alliance does when the next one lands.
  • China — the export leg weakens before the consumption leg is fixedA 49.2 PMI with new orders at a 2023 low, one day after a Politburo that promised acceleration rather than stimulus. Markets are pricing an easing window around end-Q3. If that slips, the 4.5% floor of the full-year target is genuinely in play. Watch the MATCH Act's 150-day clock on Japan and the Netherlands, and whether domestic DUV claims get independent verification.
  • Bundibugyo in the DRC — the story nobody is covering3,605 cases and 1,587 deaths as of 30 July, against 1,460 and 452 on 1 July: the count roughly doubled inside the month this log covers. Case fatality near 44%, a species the licensed countermeasures were not built for, exported cases already in France and Germany, and CDC travel restrictions renewed through mid-August. Remdesivir and MBP134 trials began 12 July and Oxford is cleared for Phase 1 — the first thing on this thread that could change the trajectory. This is still the thread on this page most likely to be leading it in a month.
  • Türkiye — the first hard number on what the war costQ2 tourism revenue fell 2.6% to $15.865bn, with H1 at $25.7bn against a full-year target of $68bn — visitors down, yield defended, and Marmaris up 22.7% in sterling terms into seventh place. That, plus a $10.4bn monthly trade deficit and Brent back near $90, against a policy rate frozen at 37% until 10 September. The Iraq pipeline rollover at 750,000 bpd is the one genuinely good piece of news, and it was signed for one year, not five. Watch the August CPI print and whether Yeni Parti registers a parliamentary group — it is still absent from the commission seat table.
  • Iceland, 29 AugustGallup 52–48, Maskína 53.1–46.9, both at or inside the error bar, early voting open since 27 July. A Yes only reopens accession talks; any treaty needs a second referendum. The first enlargement signal of the cycle.
