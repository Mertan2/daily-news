Weekly Report

Week of August 3 – 9, 2026

A running log of the week's major stories — updated each day. Revision: Thursday, August 6. Where last week left off (July 27 – August 2): the Iran war ran pause → collapse → a two-hour barrage with Saudi participation → a second pause announced late on Saturday 1 August while Kuwait was shooting down drones. Gen. Alexus Grynkewich had warned the Pentagon in writing that without another destroyer he must choose between defending the US homeland and defending Israel. Zelensky said Ukraine had stopped one ballistic missile out of 27 "simply because there are no interceptors." The Ceuta border gave way — 67 dead, ~69,500 returned to Morocco in 30 hours — and 22 EU leaders demanded an emergency interior ministers' meeting, with France, Portugal and Luxembourg declining to sign. Kumamoto's earthquake toll reached 36; Türkiye's policy rate was frozen at 37% until 10 September; China's Politburo produced no stimulus package against a 49.2 PMI. What this week has established so far: the binding constraint on both wars is the same interceptor and long-range-missile inventory, and it is now measurable — "virtually all" ATACMS gone, ~80% of THAAD, ~half of Patriot. Everything else on this page follows from that number.

Daily Log

Monday, August 3
  • Türkiye · disinflation, on paperTÜİK's July CPI came in at 31.75% annual, just under the 31.8% consensus and the lowest reading since March; monthly was 1.78%, a three-month high but below the 1.83% forecast. Housing and utilities decelerated hard (45.14% → 40.32%) and transport eased to 30.83% — but food accelerated to 37.53% from 35.45%. BIST 100 opened the week +0.64% at 13,544.32. The policy rate stays at 37% with no meeting until 10 September.
  • Venezuela · 6,125 dead, and almost no coverageNational Assembly President Jorge Rodríguez put the confirmed toll from the 24 June earthquakes — a Mw 7.2 foreshock followed thirty seconds later by a Mw 7.5 mainshock — above 6,125, with 61,000 treated in hospitals and 80% of buildings collapsed in La Guaira state. Six weeks on, this is among the deadliest earthquakes of the decade and has received a small fraction of the attention Kumamoto's 38 deaths did.
  • Europe · Italy red-alerts a record 25 citiesThe fourth heatwave since May peaked. By this point in the season EFFIS had the EU at 457,459 hectares burned (to 29 July) — 31.8% above 2025 and roughly two and a half times the 20-year average — with 17 civilians, six firefighters and two helicopter crew killed, 14 of the civilian deaths at Los Gallardos in Almería. England had recorded 2,877 heat deaths by 30 July, nearly double last year's total.
  • Guatemala · Fuego eruptsAsh, lava and pyroclastic flows from Monday morning; the volcanology institute warned of a move "towards a more explosive phase."
  • Tennis · a first for Southeast AsiaAlexandra Eala beat Jessica Pegula to take the Washington DC Open — the first WTA Tour title won by a Filipino player, and by any player from Southeast Asia.
  • AlsoBoris Nadezhdin announced he had flown to France, citing pressure on his family. Drone debris killed seven, including three children, on a beach at Gelendzhik. Floods in southern India killed at least 14. The UN reported dozens of children and civilians killed in Israeli strikes it characterises as ceasefire violations in Gaza.
Tuesday, August 4
  • The number that reframes both warsReuters reported the US Army has expended "virtually all" of its ATACMS and Precision Strike Missile stocks across five months of Operation Epic Fury, with CENTCOM drawing down inventory pre-positioned in other theatres to reload — so the global reserve is shrinking, not just the regional one. CNN put THAAD interceptor depletion near 80% of pre-war levels and Patriot near half. Even at quadrupled production, restocking runs into 2027 or beyond. Commanders have been rationing interceptors and choosing which incoming strikes to defend against — reporting ties those decisions directly to the President's pauses. Read against last week's Grynkewich memo, this is the same story with a quantity attached.
  • Europe · the ministers meet on CeutaThe extraordinary interior ministers' videoconference demanded by 22 heads of government convened, chaired by Irish Justice Minister Jim O'Callaghan. Output: unity language ("the EU is united and stands ready to support Spain"), calls for tougher action on smuggling networks, stronger returns policy and third-country cooperation. Commissioner Magnus Brunner blamed smuggling networks and social-media disinformation. Ministers put entries the previous week at roughly 72,000. No legislative answer to Spain's 29 June Supreme Court ruling barring summary sea returns; no explanation of the three non-signatories.
  • Markets · the melt-upS&P 500 +1.8% to 7,736.52, a record close; Nasdaq +2.6% to 26,584.99; Dow up roughly 700 points to a record near 53,180. Driven by AI names and Middle East de-escalation hopes.
  • Hormuz · a hit and a milestone in the same dayQatari mediators reported progress in US–Iran talks. The Liberian-flagged bulk carrier Minoan Pioneer was struck by a projectile and abandoned. Iraq confirmed exporting more than 30 million barrels through the strait between 4 July and 4 August — the first hard evidence that partial flow had resumed under the pauses.
  • Japan · the white paper names ChinaCabinet approved the 2026 Defense White Paper, warning that China is extending military reach into the Pacific beyond the First Island Chain and repeating the "unprecedented and greatest strategic challenge" formulation. Separately Jiji reported that PM Takaichi had asked BOJ Governor Ueda in May to buy JGBs if needed to hold down long rates — an unusual naming of a specific tool by a sitting prime minister, and the opening of a live independence debate.
  • Guatemala · red alertSecond-highest national alert declared for Sacatepéquez, Escuintla and Chimaltenango; eight villages evacuated, ~1,700 people in shelters, ~29,000 affected by ashfall, National Route 14 closed, lahars running up to 7 km.
  • AlsoWashington revoked the visa of Brazilian ambassador Maria Luiza Ribeiro Viotti in a reciprocal move. A mass funeral was held in Gaza City for 112 bodies recovered from an early-war strike. An Indian-flagged cargo ship sank in the Red Sea after a projectile attack; all 14 crew rescued. The RSF commander "Jebel Moon" was confirmed killed in West Darfur. Colombia's JEP charged 27 former FARC members with crimes against humanity. Advocacy and business groups sued DHS over 190-day average DACA renewal backlogs. A manhunt continued in Anaconda, Montana. The WNBA named its Cleveland expansion team the Sirens.
Wednesday, August 5
  • Ukraine · 24 ballistic missiles, zero stopped, 17 deadRussia fired 24 ballistic missiles, four cruise missiles and 115 drones at Kyiv and the surrounding region overnight. Seventeen killed, 44 wounded. Ukraine intercepted no ballistic missiles. Targets included civilian storage sites, a railway station, a brewery and logistics warehouses. This is the second consecutive week in which the interception rate for ballistics has been one or zero, and it now has a documented cause upstream: the same Patriot interceptor pool the Iran campaign is consuming. Trump promised Zelensky a domestic production licence at the July NATO summit, then told the FT he was "not sure."
  • Iran · Tehran and Muscat agree coordinatesForeign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei said Iran and Oman had agreed geographic coordinates for shipping routes through Hormuz, with a joint statement in final drafting. Reporting described a 60-day interim reopening without tolls that Washington hoped to announce the same day. Iran continued to deny direct talks with the United States. Hardliners in Tehran began compiling impeachment documentation against Foreign Minister Araghchi, with President Pezeshkian named as a possible second target. The US Treasury lifted sanctions on Fly Baghdad. Brent fell a third straight session to the $78–79 range.
  • Türkiye · the framework law reaches parliamentOne year to the day after the monitoring commission first met, the Milli Dayanışma ve Toplumsal Bütünleşmenin Güçlendirilmesine Dair Kanun Teklifi was submitted to the TBMM presidency with roughly 360 signatures from AK Parti, MHP, DEM Parti, CHP and HÜDA PAR. Twelve articles: verified dissolution and disarmament with materiel recorded; investigations suspended five years for offences under 15 years and ten years for those above; an MGK decision on the PKK's dissolution to be announced; a standing parliamentary monitoring commission.
  • Markets · the first SpaceX printDow +0.5% to another record; S&P −0.2% off Tuesday's high; Nasdaq −0.8%. SpaceX, reporting for the first time since listing, nearly doubled revenue and narrowed operating losses on Starlink and AI — and fell about 14%, with Q2 capex at $18.4bn, a sixfold increase, and roughly $101bn of shares becoming free to trade. AMD beat but guided Q3 in line and fell ~7%, compounded by Musk saying SpaceX would buy Nvidia exclusively. Disney beat by $0.20 and rose ~3%; Eli Lilly also beat. The rule from late July held: capex is rewarded when revenue proof travels with it.
  • Japan · a food tax cut clears the LDPThe LDP General Council unanimously approved cutting the consumption tax on food from 8% to 1% for two years from April 2027. The bond market's answer: the 10-year JGB yield at 2.50%, highest since July 1997, with the yen near 157 and debt around 237% of GDP.
  • Space · a rocket stage hit the MoonAt 06:35 UTC a spent ~4-tonne Falcon 9 upper stage — left over from the January 2025 Blue Ghost 1 / ispace Resilience launch — struck near Einstein Crater at 2.43 km/s. Unplanned. ESO's Very Large Telescope resolved sodium and lithium emission lines in the plume for five to ten minutes: a rare observed impact of roughly known energy, and a free calibration point for lunar exosphere models.
  • Germany · eight convictionsA Hamburg court convicted eight members of the far-right "Last Defense Wave" of attempted murder, arson and bomb attacks on asylum shelters. An explosive-laden drone was defused at Leipzig/Halle Airport. Separately, Spain and Ecuador announced 44 arrests and 21 tonnes of cocaine seized.
  • Football · Salah lands in IstanbulMohamed Salah arrived in Türkiye to complete a free transfer to Trabzonspor on a two-year deal, after mutually terminating his Liverpool contract a year early.

Threads to Watch — live through the weekend

  • The inventory is the story, and it is now a number"Virtually all" ATACMS and PrSM expended; ~80% of THAAD; ~half of Patriot; restocking into 2027 at quadrupled output; CENTCOM pulling from other theatres. On the same 24 hours, Kyiv took 24 ballistic missiles and stopped none. Two wars, one interceptor pool, and commanders openly rationing. Every diplomatic move this week — the Omani corridor, the sixth night without a strike, the 60-day no-toll framing — is more parsimoniously explained by that ledger than by any change of heart. Watch whether Washington publishes the joint statement, and watch Hormuz transit counts rather than communiqués.
  • A deal Tehran cannot call a dealIran insists it is negotiating shipping coordinates with Oman, not terms with America, while hardliners assemble impeachment paperwork against Araghchi and float the same against Pezeshkian. That is the constraint on durability: an arrangement whose Iranian domestic description must remain "a technical route agreement with a neighbour" has very little tolerance for a public American victory lap. Watch the wording of the joint statement, if it appears.
  • The AI trade's new test case: SpaceXRevenue nearly doubled, losses narrowed, and the stock fell 14% on $18.4bn of quarterly capex and a $101bn lockup expiry. AMD beat and fell on in-line guidance plus losing SpaceX to Nvidia; Disney beat and rose. The late-July rule — capex rewarded only when revenue proof travels with it — survived contact with a rocket company. Watch whether it survives Nvidia's print. See the deep dive.
  • Türkiye: disinflation on paper, record lows in the market31.75% annual CPI, the softest since March — and within three days the lira set all-time lows against the dollar, euro and sterling, with food inflation accelerating to 37.53%. The rate is frozen at 37% until 10 September, which is now the single dated event on the Turkish calendar that matters. Corporate earnings (Türk Telekom, Koç) are beating comfortably; the household-facing series are not improving.
  • The framework law's real test is the calendarRoughly 360 signatures across five parties including the CHP is a genuinely broad coalition, and the suspension architecture (5 years under 15-year offences, 10 above) is the substantive core. Two things to watch: whether the promised MGK decision on the PKK's dissolution actually issues, and whether Yeni Parti — absent from last week's commission seat table — appears anywhere in this process. The pro-government press split on Thursday's front pages is the leading indicator.
  • China: two surveys, one directionOfficial PMI 49.2 with new orders at a 2023 low; the private gauge 50.9 against 51.5 expected, with export orders contracting. The Politburo promised counter-cyclical adjustment without a package. Markets price easing around end-Q3; if it slips, the bottom of the ~5% target is live. On chips, the H200 approval has produced zero deliveries and Nvidia's China share has halved to ~50% — the damage is uncertainty, not enforcement. The tariff truce expires 10 November.
  • Japan's fiscal–monetary collisionA food tax cut from 8% to 1%, a prime minister asking the BOJ to buy bonds, a 10-year yield at a 1997 high, and 237% debt-to-GDP. Wages are finally working (six straight months of real-wage growth, a 5.3% shunto), which is precisely why the BOJ has a tightening case — and precisely why the government wants the long end capped. These cannot both be satisfied. Watch the next BOJ meeting and the JGB auction tail.
  • Bundibugyo: the largest outbreak in DRC history, still uncovered3,874 cases, 1,751 deaths, ~45% case fatality, 717 in isolation, and a species the licensed countermeasures were not built for. The count added roughly 270 cases and 165 deaths in four days. Remdesivir and MBP134 trials started 12 July and Oxford has Phase 1 clearance — still the only inputs on this thread that could bend the curve.
  • Europe's fire season is now a capacity problem, not a weather story457,459 hectares to 29 July, 2.5× the 20-year average, up to 400,000 people displaced or under evacuation order, 25 Italian cities at red alert, ~10,000 heat deaths in June alone, and nuclear plants approaching cooling-water shutdown thresholds. Note the counter-example worth explaining: Türkiye is 52% below its 20-year average at 25,701 hectares, with 258 of 260 fires since 29 July controlled.
  • Iceland, 29 AugustEarly voting is open; the latest Maskína poll (22–29 July, n=1,865) shows a slim majority for resuming accession talks — a different question from joining, and any treaty would need a second referendum. Still the first enlargement signal of this cycle.
