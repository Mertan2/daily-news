Daily Log

Monday, July 20
  • UK · Burnham takes officeAndy Burnham became prime minister — Britain's seventh in a decade, installed without a general election — and told his first cabinet he leads a "cost-of-living government," moving immediately to scrap Starmer's Digital ID programme. His cabinet took shape with John Healey as Chancellor, Ed Miliband as Foreign Secretary and Yvette Cooper to Health.
  • Türkiye · the municipal arrest wave widensSimultaneous operations across five provinces produced detention orders for 31 people, including CHP İzmit Mayor Fatma Kaplan Hürriyet and her husband, on bribery and bid-rigging charges. Opposition outlets called it a political purge — now spanning 40+ CHP municipalities since the İmamoğlu detention of March 2025; pro-government outlets called it anti-corruption.
  • S. China Sea · a Filipino sailor injured at AyunginA close-range encounter near the grounded BRP Sierra Madre at Second Thomas Shoal left a Filipino sailor injured — Manila says China Coast Guard personnel struck him with a baton; Beijing says Philippine boats rammed a Chinese vessel. The US and Australia condemned China's "destabilising and dangerous conduct," overshadowing the ASEAN meetings in Manila.
  • Türkiye · the CHP leadership case reaches the top courtThe "mutlak butlan" appeal — over the voided 2023 congress that elected Özel and the reinstatement of Kılıçdaroğlu's caretakers — reached the Court of Cassation, but the judicial recess pushes any ruling to September, freezing the party's contested "two-headed" leadership.
Tuesday, July 21
  • Türkiye · Özel splits from the CHPIn what he called his final CHP group address — "the sadness of a farewell whose time has come" — Özgür Özel announced he and loyalist deputies are leaving to form a new party, its name to be revealed only when the founding petition is filed July 27. Reports put possible defections at 75–82 deputies, which would make the new party the main opposition and drop the court-restored CHP to fourth or fifth in parliament.
  • Japan · Takaichi's ¥370tn strategy advancesThe cabinet approved its annual economic blueprint, advancing over ¥370tn (~$2.3tn) in public-private domestic investment through March 2041 — including ¥101.6tn for AI and chips — Japan's first comprehensive industrial policy in decades, against a bond market already pricing it sceptically.
  • US · Hegseth asks for $88bn as the war's bill landsSecretary of War Pete Hegseth told the Senate the Iran campaign has cost about $38bn so far and pressed Congress for a nearly $88bn supplemental. (Dollar figures single-sourced at compile time.)
  • Türkiye · Erdoğan calls the Iran strikes "unlawful"Erdoğan said the attacks on Iran are "unlawful" and that Turkey's "priority is a ceasefire and reopening dialogue"; Iran's FM credited Ankara with helping keep a new front from opening among Iranian-Kurdish groups in northern Iraq.
Wednesday, July 22
  • Iran war · an 11th night, and oil near $95CENTCOM reported an eleventh consecutive night of strikes after the June ceasefire collapsed; Trump warned Tehran would "pay" for US deaths and downplayed a mediator-floated 10-day truce. Brent surged to ~$94–95, its highest since early June, on the strikes plus Houthi claims of hitting two Saudi tankers in the Red Sea. Schumer said Democrats will force another war-powers vote.
  • Japan · the yen breaks ¥163The yen weakened past ¥163/dollar — its softest since 1986 — with FinMin Katayama "prepared to intervene." The Nikkei 225 gave up a 1,200-point morning surge to close slightly lower at 66,115.60.
  • US · Alphabet and Tesla beat, then fallAlphabet posted Q2 revenue of $119.8bn (+24%) with Google Cloud up 82% to $24.8bn, but slipped after hours on a raised capex forecast. Tesla hit record revenue of $28.24bn yet missed on profit — EPS $0.33 vs $0.53 — with operating margin down to 1.4%. The AI-capex question that broke Tokyo's chip names reached the US hyperscalers.
  • UK · inflation falls to 2.6%June CPI came in at 2.6% y/y, down from 2.8% and below forecasts, led by easing transport and food prices — feeding the Bank of England's July 30 decision.
  • Asia · Rubio and Wang Yi meet in ManilaThe two met on the ASEAN sidelines; Rubio said Xi's late-September White House visit remains on track despite Trump's China election-interference claim (which Beijing called "fabricated" and Trump walked back). A US–Korea–Japan trilateral reaffirmed deterrence against North Korea.
  • Ukraine · 13 shadow-fleet vessels claimed in 48 hoursUkraine's drone-forces commander said strikes hit 13 Russian shadow-fleet vessels over July 21–22, and drones struck Wildberries warehouses inside Russia, injuring eight. (Tally single-sourced to the Ukrainian military.)
  • Europe · Spain's worst fire of the yearA Guadalajara blaze burned an estimated 32,000 hectares, evacuating 34 municipalities, as the EU deployed a record firefighting contingent. Germany's CDU/CSU moved to replace faction chief Jens Spahn after a surrogacy row, and Brussels braced for imminent US "forced-labour" tariffs under a 15% cap.
Thursday, July 23 today
  • Türkiye · TCMB holds at 37% — and there's no August meetingThe central bank kept its one-week repo rate at 37% for a fourth straight time (overnight lending 40%, borrowing 35.5%), as unanimously expected. With no committee meeting in August, this decision governs markets until September 10; the statement pledged to hold the tight stance "until price stability is achieved" and warned July inflation may temporarily rise. The lira traded near 47.2/USD; June CPI was 32.11% y/y.

CXMT priced a ~$9.8bn Shanghai IPO, the mainland's biggest chip listing since SMIC.
