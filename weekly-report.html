Running Threads

Trump in Beijing — first U.S. state visit to China in 9 years

President Trump touches down in Beijing the evening of May 13 for a three-day state visit (May 13–15) at Xi Jinping's invitation, marking the first U.S. presidential trip to China in nearly a decade. The visit was originally set for early April but slipped over the Iran war. Agenda: trade, tariffs, the Iran war, Taiwan, rare-earth export controls and AI policy. Both sides are expected to extend the October 2025 one-year tariff truce. Trump: top topic "more than anything else will be trade." Most analysts (CSIS, CFR, Washington Post) expect détente reinforcement rather than breakthroughs — and judge that Xi enters from the stronger position, leveraging rare-earth dominance and Europe/Asia's energy pain.

Iran war & ceasefire diplomacy

Through the weekend the U.S.-mediated ceasefire was on "massive life support" after Trump rejected Iran's counterproposal as "garbage." Treasury sanctioned 12 entities for moving Iranian oil to China. Iran's parliament speaker said the IRGC is ready to "teach a lesson" to any aggressor. Pentagon comptroller pegs U.S. cost at $29B. Strait of Hormuz remains largely closed; EIA warned May 13 that crude could surge another $20/bbl if the blockage persists. Pump prices remain the chief domestic-political pressure point: ~$1.50/gal higher since U.S. entry into the war.

Russia–Ukraine: truce dead, drones surge

The U.S.-brokered May 9–11 ceasefire expired late on the 11th. Overnight May 11–12, Russia fired 216 drones at Ukraine; Ukrainian air defences neutralised 192. Nine Kyiv-region facilities damaged. Civilian casualties in Kryvyi Rih. Ukraine struck back at Russian gas-industry sites in Orenburg, 1,500 km inside Russia. FM Sybiha says Europe may broker a mutual airport-strike pause. Cumulative Russian troop losses since Feb. 24, 2022: 1,343,050.

UK political crisis

By May 12, 88 Labour MPs and four ministers had called for PM Keir Starmer to step down after Labour's heavy May local-election losses (~1,500 council seats lost, mostly to Reform UK). Health Secretary Wes Streeting backed off a formal challenge but met Starmer privately on May 12. Eurasia Group raised the probability of Starmer being ousted in 2026 to 80%. Gilt yields are climbing. Starmer pledged a "complete break," nationalising British Steel and pivoting closer to Brussels.

Markets: records, then a hot CPI

The S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 printed new all-time highs Monday May 11 (S&P 7,412.84; Nasdaq 26,274.13). On May 12, April CPI came in +0.6% m/m / +3.8% y/y — the highest annual rate since May 2023; core +0.4%/+2.8%. Energy did 40%+ of the damage. Equities pulled back (S&P 7,400.96 −0.16%; Nasdaq 26,088.20 −0.71%; Dow +0.11%). CME-implied odds of a Fed hike by year-end jumped to ~30%. Real wages −0.5% m/m. Bitcoin slid to ~$80.9k.

Hantavirus cruise-ship cluster

MV Hondius cluster (notified to WHO May 2) reached 11 cases (9 confirmed, 2 probable) and 3 deaths by May 12. Pathogen identified as Andes hantavirus — only hantavirus documented to spread person-to-person, and even then only with close prolonged contact. CDC met the ship in the Canary Islands May 7. WHO chief Tedros: "not another Covid." Risk to public is low; passengers from at least five U.S. states under monitoring. Macron says situation "under control" in France.
